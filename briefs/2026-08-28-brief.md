## Brevard City Council

### August 17, 2026, regular meeting
#### Decided
**Sight Triangle and Stormwater Management ordinance amendments.** Council approved both unanimously. Public hearings on both were held July 20, and the July 20 minutes already record unanimous approval that day, so it is unclear whether the August 17 vote is a distinct action or a repeat entry in the packet. See Unanswered questions below.

**Loitering, Trespassing, Public Intoxication and Hours of Operation ordinance.** Approved unanimously with a modification to Section 42-5(C).

**Cashiers Valley Road Sewer Extension.** Council committed $88,400 unanimously, with the specific funding source inside the Water and Sewer Fund still to be determined at the next meeting.

**Parks, Trails and Recreation Committee.** Shanna Powell was appointed unanimously.

**Presbyterian Church lease extension.** A Transylvania Times account reports Council voted to extend the ground lease for the affordable housing project on Cashiers Valley Road, though the vote count is not specified in that account and no minutes confirm it.

#### Heard, not decided
**FOG (fats, oils and grease) ordinance.** A public hearing was held on amendments to UDO Chapter 17 and Code of Ordinances Chapter 70. Council did not vote at this meeting per the available record. The city faces a DEQ compliance finding from April 2025 and potential fines of $25,000 per day for noncompliance, per the Transylvania Times.

**Remote Participation Policy for appointed boards and committees.** The Finance, HR and Citizen Appointment Committee recommended approval. Full Council consideration is still pending.

**Downtown Master Plan Committee renaming.** Proposed rename to Downtown Master Plan Implementation and Downtown Advisory Committee was before Council; no vote recorded.

#### Public comment
**Peter Offen** spoke against expanding the loitering ordinance's reach: "Don't undermine your own progress by targeting a larger population."

**Rodney Locks** said residents are confused about the loitering definitions.

**Dean Lytle** argued the response to homelessness "has to be attacked from all ends, not just law enforcement."

**Lauren Wolf** raised flooding concerns tied to the grease trap ordinance debate, per the Transylvania Times account of this meeting.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
Funding source within the Water and Sewer Fund for the $88,400 Cashiers Valley Road commitment is to be identified at the next meeting.

Whether the sight triangle and stormwater ordinance actions on August 17 represent new votes or a duplicate of the July 20 record could not be confirmed from the documents available.

#### Money
$88,400 for Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension. $5,000 FY27 impact for FOG Program Coordinator reclassification. Under $10,000 for FOG equipment and training. Approximately $88,000 in redirected funds for HAC Project parking lot utility extension. $820.46 in July property tax releases. $25,654.87 in July interest income on the city's main investment account.

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### August 24, 2026, regular meeting
Figures and vote counts below come from the Vimeo auto-generated transcript. No approved minutes exist yet for this meeting.

#### Decided
**Consent agenda**, including the Community Transportation Program application, proclamations for Constitution Week and National Preparedness Month, and several policy updates, approved unanimously per the recording.

**Comprehensive Transportation Plan**, adopted with a modification substituting a Wilson Road extension for a previously planned Railroad Avenue extension, unanimous per the recording.

**Personnel Board appointments**: Lisa Sheffield, Amber Webb, and Shane Hooper, unanimous per the recording.

**Council on Aging reappointments**: Kay Parker, Brenda Skeffington, Mary Short, and Deborah Yunker, unanimous per the recording.

**Sierra Lawing appointed** review officer for the county by resolution, unanimous per the recording.

**Conestee Fire and Rescue contract amendment**, moving to a monthly reimbursement model with new fiscal controls for a minimum of 18 months, unanimous per the recording.

**FY26 fourth-quarter budget amendments** approved and the year-end financial summary received, unanimous per the recording.

**Budget amendment for financial software**, $8,000 (verify) for a Tyler Technologies fund conversion and $21,600 (verify) for Open Finance implementation, combining to roughly $29,600 (verify), unanimous per the recording.

**Public safety tower grants accepted**, USDA Rural Development and Bureau of Justice Assistance awards totaling approximately $2.3 million (verify), with an additional $397,860 (verify) allocated from assigned fund balance to cover remaining construction costs, unanimous per the recording.

#### Heard, not decided
NCDOT's Daniel Sellers and Land of Sky RPO's Vicky Eastland presented on the SPOT/STI prioritization process explaining why the Highway 64 widening from Israel Road to Brevard scored low, and committed to a future presentation on that scoring plus the Brevard High School pump station funding shortfall (roughly $1 million, verify).

#### Public comment
**Samantha Jones** described the death of her son two years ago in an incident involving two boys and alcohol, said no comprehensive investigation followed, and asked commissioners for help pursuing justice.

**Ashley Hart** opposed FLOCK license plate reader cameras, said the county sheriff's office shares data with at least 15 other agencies with no public transparency portal, and asked for a policy review.

**Robert Jaworski (verify)** described ALPR systems as broader surveillance tools, citing wrongful stops in other states, and quoted Asheville's mayor calling to end that city's FLOCK contract.

**Doug Powell** spoke on Constitution Week, the Fourth Amendment's origins, and quoted Franklin on liberty and security: "I prefer dangerous freedom over peaceful slavery."

**Karen Gleason** asked the full board to issue a written position on data centers, referencing an earlier statement by Commissioner Dalton she read as opposition.

**Ashley Hart**, in the second comment period, urged commissioners to act on Samantha Jones's account rather than dismiss it.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
Finance Director Megan O'Neill committed to reviewing Conestee Fire and Rescue's new policies before the contract amendment executes, and to meeting with CFR on September 17.

Communications Director Cameron Sexton committed to reporting back on public safety tower site locations once secured, and to exploring HAM/GMRS capability on the new towers.

County staff committed to beginning compilation of Hurricane Helene funding documentation for a state auditor request due October 13, a deadline that may be extended; Megan O'Neill was to follow up on that possible extension by September 1.

School Bond Project Manager Beecher Allison committed to presenting the Rosman-area construction sequence at the September 28 meeting.

Commissioner Chapman asked for a sheriff's briefing on FLOCK camera usage, coverage, and results before the board takes further action; no date was set.

#### Money
$381,381 (verify) estimated annual county impact from federal SNAP administrative and benefit cost shifts, split as $172,640 (verify) administrative and $208,741 (verify) benefits, equivalent to about 0.31 cents (verify) on the tax rate. $2.3 million (verify) in new tower grants. $397,860 (verify) in county fund balance for tower construction. $29,600 (verify) in financial software costs.

#### Disagreements
On FLOCK cameras: Ashley Hart, Robert Jaworski, and Doug Powell called the system unconstitutional surveillance. Commissioner Chapman said he wants a factual sheriff briefing before deciding, citing cases where FLOCK helped solve crimes. Commissioner McKelvey said he would vote no if it comes before him, citing civil liberties.

On data centers: Karen Gleason wants a written board position opposing them. Commissioner Chapman said the county lacks the inventory to attract large centers but should stay open to smaller ones. Burton Hodges of the Transylvania Economic Alliance said the county has self-selected out of the industry due to power infrastructure limits.

### July 13, 2026, regular meeting
Minutes for this meeting were approved and became available this period, roughly six weeks after the meeting, consistent with the county's normal lag.

#### Decided

**FY 2026 audit contract** with Martin Starnes and Associates, base fee $83,000, not to exceed $124,500, unanimous.

#### Heard, not decided

No board vote on the recommendation is recorded in these minutes.

Peter Offen said the board should act more promptly on major projects. Owen Carson called recent challenges warning signs requiring proactive leadership. State Representative Anna Ferguson addressed the audience on infrastructure funding status, saying "the issue was not the result of any error by Transylvania County," referring to a state reallocation of Hurricane Helene funds.

#### Money
$10,000,000 in state infrastructure funds originally allocated to the county were reallocated by the state, with $4,000,000 later restored as Hurricane Helene recovery funding. $114,515.85 Allies 4 Outcomes carryforward. $83,000 to $124,500 audit contract. Approximately $215,000 in savings from the ThreatLocker agreement.
