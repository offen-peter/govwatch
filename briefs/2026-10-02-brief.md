## Brevard City Council

### September 21, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Stop sign ordinance.** Council approved unanimously an amendment to the city traffic schedule adding a stop sign at Thomas Street and West Main Street, per the agenda packet. No readable minutes exist to confirm the vote independently.
- **UDO Chapter 17 amendments.** Council unanimously adopted amendments to the Unified Development Ordinance's fats, oils and grease program, per the agenda packet.
- **Ecusta Trail Advisory Board appointment.** Council unanimously appointed Joseph Moore as an at-large member, per the agenda packet.

#### Heard, not decided
- **UDO building height amendments.** Council held a public hearing on proposed changes to Chapters 2, 6, and 19 governing building height; no public comment was offered. Council is expected to act at the October 5 meeting.
- **Off-street parking minimum removal.** Council held a public hearing on proposed changes to Chapters 2, 8, 10, and 17 removing off-street parking minimums; no public comment was offered. A new state law (2026-39) prohibits parking minimums statewide effective January 1, 2027, regardless of council's vote.
- **SRF loan applications.** Council discussed resolutions to apply for State Revolving Fund loan or grant assistance for the Azalea/Rhododendron sewer extension and for Capital Improvements Program Priority #1 sewer system improvements, estimated at $3.3 million.
- **Silversteen Park pavilion and bathroom.** Final design was discussed; the project's original private donation funding is exhausted, leaving construction funding (roughly $415,000 per a later estimate) unresolved.
- **City trail naming.** Council discussed a Parks, Trails and Recreation Committee recommendation on naming conventions for city trails, including the Estatoe and Ecusta trails.
- **Rosenwald Fund (Fund 82) surplus.** With $88,969 available, staff were directed to bring back a menu of funding options, including for the Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension.
- **Downtown parking study.** Staff presented results from a community survey of 817 participants (93 percent local) and listening sessions with about 30 stakeholders, estimating potential parking revenue of $88,000 to $417,500 annually depending on the management alternative chosen.
- **Closed session.** Council held a closed session on an economic development matter under NCGS 143-318.11(a)(4).

#### Public comment
- **Doug Powell** spoke on Constitution Week and founding principles, per the city's agenda packet: "our freedoms are not guaranteed; they must be understood and protected."
- **Jimmy Veal** raised concerns about Pinnacle Road property access being blocked by trail infrastructure, warning the city's liability exposure, per the agenda packet.

#### Money

- $350,000 increase to the Mills Avenue culvert repair capital project, tied to FEMA reimbursement.

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### September 28, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
All items below are per the meeting recording (auto-generated captions), not yet confirmed by approved minutes, which typically lag four to six weeks.
- **Duke Energy tower variance.** The Board voted unanimously, per the recording, to approve a variance allowing a 330-foot telecommunications tower against the county's 180-foot ordinance limit, following a quasi-judicial hearing. The county Planning Board had unanimously recommended denial on August 20 but indicated it would support the variance if it had the authority to grant one.
- **Consent agenda** approved unanimously, per the recording.
- **Board of Adjustment and Appeals.** Alan McAlado reappointed, unanimous, per the recording (name spelling unconfirmed).
- **Parks and Recreation Commission.** Eric Rasmussen and Jessica Huey reappointed, unanimous, per the recording; the county continues advertising for remaining vacancies.
- **Connestee Fire Rescue contract.** The Board executed an amended contract moving to monthly reimbursement based on documentation, with a minimum 18-month term, unanimous, per the recording.
- **Helene public assistance agents.** Kevin Shook and Cameron Sexton were confirmed as the county's designated agents for FEMA public assistance projects, unanimous, per the recording.
- **Courthouse bond financing, preliminary.** The Board gave preliminary approval to negotiate limited obligation bonds for courthouse construction, reported as up to $40 million (verify), unanimous per the recording. Final approval is expected at the October meeting, with pricing in November and issuance in December.
- **Federal legislative goals.** The Board voted unanimously, per the recording, to submit federal legislative goal proposals to the NC Association of County Commissioners, due October 2.

#### Heard, not decided
- **CDBG-I grant application.** A public hearing was held on the county's application for Community Development Block Grant-Infrastructure funding, seeking not to exceed $1 million toward a roughly $1 million funding gap for the Brevard High School sewer line and pump station. The application deadline was September 30; the state's funding decision is pending.
- **Hazard mitigation grant acquisitions.** Eleven properties remain under review for FEMA buyout; six were found ineligible, nine in a first group are preparing acquisition proposals. No final list has been adopted.

## Cross-cutting threads

- **Surveillance cameras (Flock/ALPR).** Brevard's police chief withdrew a request to deploy automated license plate readers after high turnout and pushback at the city's September 8 meeting (outside this reporting period, noted here for context). The county Board of Commissioners then spent a large share of its September 28 meeting on the same technology, with thirteen speakers raising similar constitutional, data-sharing, and transparency concerns about the sheriff's existing Flock cameras, and commissioners noting they have no budget control over the sheriff beyond the annual cycle.
- **Downtown Brevard Circulation Study.** Both city and county officials are engaged with this study: county commissioners attended an informational meeting ahead of an August 31 public session, and Brevard's Downtown Advisory Committee is recommending the city formally ask that a "one-way pair" option be dropped, a recommendation headed to the October 5 council meeting.
- **Sewer funding tied to state and federal grants.** Brevard is pursuing SRF loans for the Azalea/Rhododendron extension and a $3.3 million CIP sewer project, while the county is separately seeking CDBG-I grant funding for a $1 million gap in the Brevard High School sewer pump station project, both competing for overlapping state infrastructure funding programs.
