## Brevard City Council

### September 21, 2026, regular meeting
Note on sourcing: several items below come from the October 5 agenda packet, which carries draft minutes of this meeting for later council adoption. No separately confirmed signed minutes were available for this meeting, so vote counts are as recorded in that packet, not from approved minutes.

#### Decided
- **Stop sign ordinance.** Council unanimously amended the traffic schedule to add a stop sign at Thomas St and W Main St, per the packet record.
- **FOG program amendment.** Council unanimously adopted UDO Chapter 17 amendments establishing the Fats, Oils and Grease program, tied to DEQ wastewater permit requirements.
- **Ecusta Trail Advisory Board appointment.** Joseph Moore was unanimously appointed as an at-large member.
- **Property tax releases.** Council unanimously approved $2,749.53 in Tax Year 2026 releases.
- **Police Accreditation Grant.** Council unanimously approved Budget Amendment 27-01, accepting $60,700 in grant funds (full grant value $131,600).
- **Azalea/Rhododendron Sewer Extension.** Council unanimously authorized an SRF loan/grant application for this affordable-housing-linked sewer project.
- **Silversteen Park pavilion and bathroom design.** Council unanimously approved the final design; the private donation that funded design work has been exhausted, and construction funding (estimated $415,000) is not yet secured.
- **Trail naming conventions.** Council unanimously adopted naming for the Estatoe Trail and Ecusta Trail.
- **CIP Priority 1 Sewer System.** Council unanimously authorized an SRF loan application for an estimated $3,230,000 project.
- **Mini-Brooks Exemption, Headwaters Engineering.** Council approved a $22,500 streambank restoration design contract; the packet does not record a vote tally for this item.

#### Heard, not decided
- **UDO building height amendments (Chapters 2, 6, 19).** Public hearing held with no public participation. Council action deferred to a later meeting.
- **UDO parking minimum removal (Chapters 2, 8, 10, 17).** Public hearing held with no public participation. Council action deferred; a new state law (2026-39) prohibits local parking minimums effective January 1, 2027 regardless of council's vote.
- **Carolina Avenue traffic calming.** A $14,000 allocation from Fund 82 (Rosenwald Fund) was listed without a recorded vote; it appears again on the October 5 agenda for consideration.
- **ABC Board appointment.** James Thomas Kilgore's appointment was listed without a recorded vote as of this meeting.
- **One-way pair removal from Downtown Circulation Study.** A council resolution request was listed without a recorded vote.
- **Automated License Plate Readers (ALPRs).** This item was withdrawn from the agenda at the police chief's request following a September 1 public input session and heavy turnout; per the packet, Chief Wentzell said "building trust within the community is more important to me than winning the approval of technology." This context stems from the September 8 meeting, outside this reporting period, but was carried into materials reviewed this period.

#### Public comment
- **Doug Powell** spoke on Constitution Week and the founders' Fourth Amendment concerns, as recorded in materials tied to this meeting.
- **Jimmy Veal** raised concerns about Pinnacle Road property access being blocked by trail infrastructure, saying the city's liability "far exceeds what it would take to fix it."
- **Ashley Hart** (per the recording of the related September 8 session, outside this reporting period but referenced in this period's packet) said, "We the people are where it begins and we're also where it ends," addressing Rosenwald Fund allocation and ALPR concerns.
- **Councilman Baker** was quoted saying "uncomfortable moments are often the most important ones on Council," and separately, regarding the ALPR withdrawal, "We need to close this chapter out, we need to do what's right, and I'm not sure what that is right now."

#### Money
- $60,700 Police Accreditation Grant (of $131,600 total award).
- $3,230,000 estimated CIP Priority 1 sewer project, SRF loan application.
- $22,500 Mini-Brooks exemption contract for streambank restoration.
- $2,749.53 in property tax releases.
- $14,000 from Fund 82 proposed for Carolina Avenue traffic calming, not yet voted.
- $88,969 total available in the Rosenwald Fund (Fund 82) surplus, with a menu of options still pending staff follow-up.

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### August 24, 2026, regular meeting
These minutes were approved and became available during this reporting period, roughly four weeks after the meeting.

#### Decided
- **Consent agenda**, including prior meeting minutes, tax actions, several proclamations, and multiple program approvals, was adopted unanimously.
- **FY 2028 Public Transportation Program application and NCDOT funding agreement** was approved unanimously, with $166,689 administrative, $125,000 capital, and $110,000 operating funding, and a $92,503 total local match.
- **St. Philip's grant application** for the Silver Squirrels Medical Transportation voucher program was authorized unanimously, seeking $10,000.
- **Soil and Water EWP engineering supplementation** was approved unanimously, up to $25,000 from Special Projects funds.
- **Health Department bad debt write-off** of $286.20 was approved unanimously.
- **Flu vaccine fee adjustments** to $25 standard and $125 high-dose were approved unanimously.
- **Comprehensive Transportation Plan** was adopted as modified, substituting the Wilson Road Extension for the Railroad Avenue Connector.
- **FY 2026 Fourth Quarter budget amendments** (60 through 77) were approved unanimously.
- **Tyler Technologies financial software upgrade**, including the Open Finance public transparency platform, was approved unanimously at $29,600 from unassigned fund balance.
- **Public safety communication tower grants** totaling $2,300,000 were accepted, with the County covering $397,860 from assigned fund balance.
- **Personnel Board appointments** of Lisa Sheffield, Amber Webb, and Shane Hooper were approved unanimously.
- **Council on Aging reappointments** of Kae Parker, Brenda Skeffington, Mary Shore, and Debra Yunker were approved unanimously.
- **Connestee Fire Rescue contract amendment**, moving to monthly reimbursement based on documentation for a minimum 18 months, was approved unanimously.

#### Public comment
- **Samantha Jones** addressed the Board regarding the investigation into her son's death and questioned a delay in the 911 call response.
- **Ashley Hart** raised concerns about the Flock Safety automated license plate reader system, including data retention and sharing without a public transparency portal.
- **Robert Jazowski** raised Fourth Amendment concerns and cited examples of ALPR misidentification in other communities.
- **Doug Powell** spoke on Constitution Week and the founders' Fourth Amendment concerns regarding writs of assistance.
- **Karen Gleasman** requested a formal written Board statement on the county's data center position following Commissioner Dalton's departure.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- Board members Commissioner McKelvey and Vice Chair Chapman stated openness to hearing from the Sheriff on Flock camera use and data-sharing arrangements, but no hearing was scheduled as of this meeting.

#### Money
- FY 2026 General Fund revenues were reported at $76,600,000 against expenditures of $72,700,000, with an anticipated FY 2026 surplus of $4,000,000.
- $1,500,000 USDA Rural Development grant and $800,000 Bureau of Justice Assistance grant fund the communication tower project, with a $2,697,860 total estimated cost.
- $18,000,000 in school bond funding remains to be issued, with planning for a possible second bond phase beginning by fall 2028.

#### Schedule changes
- The Board's September 14, 2026 regular meeting was cancelled. The next regular meeting was set for September 28, 2026.

### September 28, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Duke Energy telecommunications tower variance.** Board voted unanimous aye, per the recording, to approve a 330-foot tower versus the county's 180-foot ordinance limit, following a quasi-judicial hearing.
- **Consent agenda** was approved unanimous aye per the recording.
- **Board and commission reappointments:** Alan McAlado to the Brevard Board of Adjustment and Appeals, and Eric Rasmussen and Jessica Huey to the Parks and Recreation Commission, with continued advertising for remaining vacancies, all unanimous aye per the recording.
- **Connestee Fire Rescue contract.** Amended payment terms, monthly reimbursement for a minimum of 18 months, were executed, unanimous aye per the recording.
- **Helene public assistance agents.** Kevin Shook and Cameron Sexton were confirmed as designated agents for FEMA public assistance projects, unanimous aye per the recording.
- **Courthouse bond financing, preliminary step.** The Board gave preliminary approval, unanimous aye per the recording, to negotiate limited obligation bonds up to $40 million (verify) for courthouse construction, with final approval expected at the October meeting.
- **Federal legislative goal proposals.** The Board approved submission to NCACC, unanimous aye per the recording, due October 2, 2026.

#### Heard, not decided
- **CDBG-I grant application for Brevard High School sewer.** A public hearing was held on the application, seeking not to exceed $1 million toward a $1 million construction funding gap for the pump station project; the application deadline was September 30, 2026, with the outcome pending state decision.
- **Hazard mitigation grant program acquisitions.** Status update on 11 properties under review; nine in "group one" are preparing acquisition proposals and six have been found ineligible. No final disposition decided.

#### Public comment
- **Joan Hardy** asked whether multiple shorter towers would be more reliable than one tall tower and about icing maintenance procedures, drawing on past local icing problems with towers.
- **Janelle Arceneaux**, a nearby property owner, objected to the tower variance as breaking the county's 180-foot height commitment, disputed the fall-distance calculations, and raised propane tank safety and aesthetic concerns.
- **Peter Often** sought clarification that courthouse bond payments, while secured by property rather than a general obligation pledge, will still come from tax dollars, and separately detailed Flock data-sharing scale across law enforcement agencies and contrasted it with the city's public input process on ALPRs.
- **Owen Carson** raised concern about students attending Brevard High School if the pump station fails, criticized an earlier decision not to retain a prior $10 million state grant, and separately argued the Board has power to compel public process on Flock cameras even without control over sheriff spending; in a further comment he raised landfill sediment runoff into Browns Mill Creek and asked the county to pause landfill work and reinstate the Transylvania Natural Resources Council.
- **Joan Hardy** also raised cybersecurity concerns about Flock systems, citing risk of access by foreign adversaries and local misuse risk against police officers.
- **Ashley Hart** detailed the sheriff's Flock transparency portal figures, stated the sheriff had twice denied public records requests for audits, and in a separate comment objected to a camera placed near Democratic Party headquarters for roughly 1.5 years, calling the placement "wrong."
- **Doug Powell** argued AI mass surveillance violates Fourth Amendment rights, drawing on 1984 and offering copies to the Board.
- **Jason Ball** asked for a public hearing from the sheriff on the Flock system and suggested prioritizing deputy pay over camera funding.
- **Robert Jazowski** said he moved from New York partly to escape a surveillance state, and stated he does not condone destruction of the cameras but wants their legal removal.
- **David Morrow** thanked commissioners for the new audio-visual equipment, noting the screens and speaker system were a significant upgrade, though font size was still sometimes too small.
- An **unidentified speaker** referenced a 2025 commissioner meeting slide on "AI in law enforcement" and warned about predictive, pre-crime policing patterns.

- **Automated license plate readers (Flock/ALPR).** Both the city and the county faced sustained public comment on ALPR surveillance this period.
