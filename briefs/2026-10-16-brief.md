## Brevard City Council

### August 17, 2026, regular meeting

Minutes for this meeting were only retrieved during this reporting period.

#### Decided
**Road closures.** Council unanimously adopted Ordinance 2026-37 closing streets for the Plein Air Festival (September 19) and HallowenFest (October 24).
**Financial depositories.** Council unanimously adopted Resolution 2026-42 reaffirming official financial depositories.
**Presbyterian Church lease.** Council unanimously authorized the city manager to sign a lease extension to June 2030.
**Development partner process.** Council voted 4 to 1 (Holder opposed) to authorize a non-competitive process for selecting a development partner for the church site.
**Remote participation.** Council unanimously adopted Resolution 2026-43 letting appointed board members participate remotely with 24-hour notice.
**Committee renaming.** Council unanimously adopted Resolution 2026-44 renaming the Downtown Master Plan Committee.

#### Public comment
**Butch Stiebing** said of the Presbyterian Church site proposal, "Now it is a planned high-rise apartment complex."
**Carol Hamann** raised concerns about the site's greenspace: "Those animals, birds, trees and vegetation there is a beautiful greenspace."
**Lauren Wise** spoke in support of the housing proposal: "Everyone is allowed to be in Brevard as far as I'm concerned."
**Ken Hynes** asked about business owner costs during the FOG program public hearing.
**Doug Powell** raised Fourth Amendment concerns about the proposed automated license plate reader program.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
**Church site assessment.** DFI's assessment of the Greenway Residential proposal was expected to take about one month, with a possible third-party review if favorable.
**ALPR town hall.** A public input session on automated license plate readers was set for September 1, 2026, with additional sessions planned, including one in Pisgah Forest.

#### Money
**Lease study cost.** The city spent $11,000 on a geotechnical analysis for the Presbyterian Church property.
**Potential review cost.** A third-party review of the development proposal was estimated at $10,000 to $15,000.
**FOG penalties.** Civil penalty tiers for the FOG program range from $500 up to $25,000 depending on tier.

### October 14, 2026, joint Downtown Master Plan and Parks, Trails & Recreation Committee meeting

This was a joint meeting replacing both committees' regular monthly sessions, rescheduled from October 21.

#### Heard, not decided
**Ecusta Trail downtown connection.** Both committees discussed routing the trail into downtown via a bike lane on W. Main Street, with options including a $25,000 to $50,000 in-house restriping job up to a $250,000 to $400,000 full repave-and-curb-cut project. Straw votes were planned but outcomes were not in the agenda record.

#### Money

**Parks and Recreation Fund balance.** That fund had $0 available.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments

## Cross-cutting threads

**Ecusta Trail funding and routing.** County-level funding for the trail's construction was confirmed per an October 2 press report, with bids expected summer 2027. That same week, city committees began deciding how to route the trail through downtown, a decision now tied to a $35,000 city fund balance against options costing up to $400,000.

**County commissioner race and county finances.** An October 13 press report on commissioner candidates referenced the county's $500,000 to $600,000 annual solid waste subsidy and roughly $1 million in courthouse construction costs tied to city zoning compliance, both issues the sitting board has pending before it (courthouse financing hearing, September 28).
