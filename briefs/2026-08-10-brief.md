### August 3, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
**Agenda amended.** Council removed the Municipal Service District Task Force Proposal and added the Cashers Valley Road Sewer Extension item, to meet a same-day grant deadline.

**Hillview Street Culvert Replacement contract.** Council awarded a $1,108,748 construction contract to Hyatt Pipeline, LLC, fully funded by FEMA, unanimous per the agenda packet.

**Deavor Road rezoning.** Two parcels were rezoned to the PGX district for CDP Aloe Properties LLC, unanimous per the agenda packet.

**Bracken Preserve trail naming.** The fourth trail was named "Mountain Rush," unanimous per the agenda packet.

**Food Vendor Pilot Program.** Adopted with a $100 permit fee, running through December 31, 2026, unanimous per the agenda packet.

**Wastewater Treatment Plant Upgrade.** Council approved an $8,575,000 capital project amendment funded by an NCDEQ Session Law 2023-134 earmark, unanimous per the agenda packet.

**ABC Board.** Lisa Conner was reappointed; staff was directed to recruit a second member with retail, restaurant, or advisory-board experience.

**UDO amendments adopted.** Sight Triangle Regulations (Chapters 9, 19, and Code Chapter 38) and Stormwater Management Development Regulations (Chapters 6, 17, 19, and Code Chapter 38) were adopted following the July 20 public hearings, as heard on the recording.

**Chapter 42 ordinance adopted with amendment.** Council adopted changes to the loitering, trespassing, public intoxication, and hours-of-operation ordinance, modifying Section 42-5 to remove "loitering generally" from the title and to require that conduct obstruct or interfere with passage. Vote reported as "motion carries," as heard on the recording; exact tally unconfirmed.

**Letter of interest to county on old courthouse.** Council approved sending a letter of interest to the county regarding a feasibility study of the old courthouse as a potential city hall, as heard on the recording.

**Cashers Valley Road sewer extension funding.** Council approved an $88,400 funding strategy from city sources, with specific sources to be determined; vote reported as "motion passes," as heard on the recording.

**Parks, Trails and Recreation Committee.** Shauna Powell was appointed, as heard on the recording.

#### Heard, not decided
**Municipal Service District Task Force Proposal** was pulled from the agenda after Heart of Brevard requested inclusion in a two-phase process; no new date set.

#### Public comment
**Peter Offen** urged caution on criminalizing civil offenses, warned that criminal charges block access to affordable housing, and raised privacy concerns about license plate reader cameras, as heard on the recording.

**Rodney Locks** asked for clarification on the definition of "habitual loafing," alcohol permit rules outside the social district, and concealed-carry distinctions between county and city property, as heard on the recording.

**Doug Powell** argued that firm enforcement of quality-of-life ordinances is compassionate and necessary, citing a Ventura, California example, as heard on the recording.

**James Beal** raised concerns that the lower 200 feet of Bracken Trail cut off vehicle access for six of nine Pinnacle Road properties and asked the council to review the eminent domain process, as heard on the recording.

**Adam Perkins**, representing Heart of Brevard, asked to be included in both phases of the MSD task force process, as heard on the recording.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
Council members asked for a precise definition of "habitual loafing" and how officers will distinguish loitering from lawful assembly; Chief Wentzell did not provide a definition at the meeting, as heard on the recording.

Council asked which city-owned properties will be affected by the firearms prohibition; no list was presented, as heard on the recording.

City Manager Wilson Hooper committed to bringing back specific funding-source options for the $88,400 sewer extension amount, by August 17, 2026 (verify), as heard on the recording.

Chief Wentzell committed to quarterly and annual reports on Chapter 42 enforcement outcomes, as heard on the recording.

Councilman Baker asked how many of the 182 July service calls represent repeat contacts with the same individuals; not answered at the meeting, as heard on the recording.
