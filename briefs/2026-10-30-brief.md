### October 26, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Fund 82 split between Carver Street and Silversteen Park.** Council voted 3-2 to send the remainder of Fund 82, reported as $41,000 to $41,469 depending on source, to seed Silversteen Park Pavilion funding, after funding $8,500 in Carver Street paving and $25,000 for utility pole removal.
- **Municipal Infrastructure Reimbursement Agreements ordinance.** Approved unanimously.
- **Fitch Irick Lease Amendment.** Approved unanimously, extending the option period through December 31, 2027.
- **Burrell Avenue right-of-way.** Council unanimously directed staff to negotiate relinquishment of an unused portion.
- **IMD Feasibility Study Grant.** Council voted 4-1 to apply for a grant to study an Asheville Highway greenway.
- **Low-Income Homeowner Assistance Program.** Approved unanimously, accepting a $60,000 Dogwood Health Trust grant.
- **September property tax releases.** Approved unanimously, totaling $52.44.
- **Joe Smith Day.** Mayor Copelof proclaimed October 23, 2026 as Joe Smith Day and presented a Key to the City.

#### Public comment
- **Randy Lytle**, Rosenwald Community Advisory Board co-chair, supported the board's unanimous recommendation for the three-way Fund 82 split.
- **Rodney Locks** of S. Rice Street commented on Rosenwald Fund 82 infrastructure eligibility.
- **Ella Jones** of McMinn Avenue spoke in favor of the Silversteen Park Pavilion, Carver Street paving, and pole removal.
- **Altha Mills Gordon** of Hillview Circle asked again for speed bumps, citing a multi-generational petition effort.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- **Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension funding.** Flagged for further discussion with no date set.
- **Silversteen Park Pavilion construction funding.** Estimated at $500,000, with only $41,469 in seed money and a $43,700 private donation secured so far; a task force was directed to develop a funding strategy.

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### October 26, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
All items below are per the meeting recording; vote tallies are reported as unanimous in the auto-generated captions and should be treated as unconfirmed until minutes are approved.
- **Consent agenda.** Approved.
- **Library Board of Trustees.** Yvonne Croca appointed.
- **Council on Aging.** Jennifer B. Henley appointed. A motion to nominate public commenter Tom Sweeney instead died for lack of a second.
- **Tourism Development Authority.** Reappointments of Dion Hodgson and Norrie Lebrun, with Jessica Whitmire named chair.
- **NCACC voting delegate.** Commissioner Larry Chapman appointed to represent the county at the November 19-20 conference.
- **Courthouse financing.** Resolution authorizing limited obligation bonds, reported as $40 million (verify), approved. Repayment is structured over 20 years.
- **Debt service fund.** Budget Ordinance Schedule B6 approved, transferring $5.5 million from the general fund to establish the new fund; this is a transfer of existing funds, not new money.
- **FY2027 first-quarter budget amendments.** Approved.

#### Heard, not decided
- **School funding gap.** Staff presented a shortfall reported as $1,840,943 (verify), split between an $857,000 local need and a $984,000 state shortfall, driven by salary and benefit increases, a retirement rate hike, special education costs, and child nutrition funding. No board action was taken; staff were directed to gather more information ahead of a joint meeting with the Board of Education targeted for early January 2027.
- **Pre-K funding responsibility.** Commissioner Chappell questioned whether the county's statutory responsibility extends to pre-K or only K-12, and whether the roughly $227,000 (verify) annual per-classroom cost was an appropriate use of county dollars. County Manager Lauder said pre-K is a county option, not a requirement, and noted funds could be shifted to K-12 if the board chose.
- **Charter school enrollment losses.** Dr. Lisa Fletcher said state law requires local dollars to follow students who leave for charter schools, and that the district gets no state funding for students who move from homeschool to a charter. Commissioners asked for a breakdown of how many lost students went to charter versus private schools; that breakdown was not available at this meeting.
- **ALPR camera surveillance.** Several public commenters asked the board to hold a hearing on license plate reader cameras. Chair McCall said no hearing will be held, calling camera policy the sheriff's constitutional prerogative and outside the board's authority to direct.
- **Capital projects update.** Beecher Allison reviewed the school bond program, including a Rosman-area groundbreaking described as "this Thursday" in the recording (reported here as October 29, 2026, verify), ongoing design work on the Brevard High School CTE project, and a Rosman Elementary permit targeted for early February 2027.
- **Q1 FY27 financial report.** Finance Director Megan O'Neal reported general fund revenues of $19.9 million and expenditures of $16.7 million for the quarter, per the recording.
- **Allies for Outcomes contract.** DSS Director Amanda Vanderhoff reported $45,664 spent of $114,515 authorized for child-welfare staffing in the first quarter.

#### Public comment
- **Ashley Hart** cited a recent federal ruling against warrantless ALPR searches and figures on out-of-state agency searches of North Carolina camera networks, asking the board to ban mass surveillance of county residents.
- **Tom Sweeney** presented his qualifications for the Council on Aging seat, citing prior service on a South Carolina governor's council on aging; he was later nominated from the floor but the motion died without a second.
- **Robert Czajkowski** challenged the "kidnapped child" justification for surveillance cameras, citing FBI figures on child abduction rates versus other childhood risks.
- **Doug Powell** argued against reliance on judicial review of constitutional questions, citing Jefferson, Reagan, and Jay, and urged the board to interpret the Fourth Amendment itself.
- **Owen Carson** praised a Blue Ridge Community College award, then criticized commissioners for joking about a Raleigh legislative trip immediately after the school funding presentation. Commissioners Dalton and Chapman responded that they have spent years building relationships with legislators in Raleigh.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- How many of the 102 students lost in FY25-26, and the 42 lost in FY26-27, went to charter schools versus private schools versus other causes: Dr. Fletcher committed to providing this before the joint meeting.
- What the 12 positions absorbed by the school system over the past two years were, and how many ESSER-funded positions remain in the local budget: Dr. Fletcher committed to reporting this before the joint meeting.
- Whether a formal letter to state legislators on courthouse funding, mentioned by Commissioner Chapman, has actually been sent.
- What the final courthouse project budget will be; commissioners are to consider this in November 2026.
- County Manager Lauder committed to compiling commissioner questions on school funding "over the next few weeks" ahead of the January joint meeting.

#### Money
- Courthouse bond: $40 million (verify), 20-year repayment.
- Debt service fund transfer: $5.5 million, from existing general fund balance.
- School funding gap: $1,840,943 (verify) for FY27, with an additional $400,000 (verify) gap anticipated next year from enrollment decline.
- Special education shortfall: $652,000, citing a state funding cap that has not been updated since 1993.
- Pre-K local shortfall: $227,000 (verify), roughly $57,000 per classroom.
- Federal radio tower grants: $2.3 million, with county match already approved.
- Stream bank restoration grants: $2.5 million awarded to the Soil and Water Conservation District.
- DSS Allies for Outcomes contract: $45,664 spent of $114,515 authorized this quarter.
