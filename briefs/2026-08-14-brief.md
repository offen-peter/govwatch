## Brevard City Council

### August 3, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
**Agenda modified.** Council removed the Municipal Service District Task Force proposal and added the Cashers Valley Road Sewer Extension item, approved unanimously, per the meeting recording.
**Minutes approved.** Council approved minutes of the April 10, 2026 budget workshop and the July 20, 2026 regular meeting.
**Consent agenda approved,** covering property tax releases and Public Safety and Finance Committee minutes, per the meeting recording. The same evening's agenda packet lists a broader set of consent items, including a Hillview Street Culvert construction contract, a Deavor Road rezoning, trail naming, the food vendor pilot program, an ABC Board reappointment, and a wastewater treatment plant capital amendment. These match items already recorded as decided at the July 20 meeting, and the discrepancy could not be resolved from the supplied records; see Gaps.
**Sight triangle regulations adopted.** Council adopted amendments to UDO Chapters 9 and 19 and Code Chapter 38, unanimous.
**Stormwater management regulations adopted.** Council adopted amendments to UDO Chapters 6, 17, 19 and Code Chapter 38, unanimous.
**Loitering, trespassing, public intoxication ordinance adopted.** Council approved amendments to Code Chapter 42 with a modification to Section 42-5(C) requiring language that conduct must obstruct or interfere with passage, motion carried, per the meeting recording.
**Cashers Valley Road sewer funding committed.** Council approved an $88,400 funding commitment from city sources, per the meeting recording; the specific funding source (Water and Sewer Fund, Rosenwald fund, or maintenance budget) was left open for the next meeting.
**Old courthouse letter of interest approved.** Council approved sending a letter of interest to the county regarding a feasibility study of the old courthouse as a potential city hall.
**Appointment.** Shauna Powell was appointed to the Parks, Trails and Recreation Committee.

#### Heard, not decided
**MSD Task Force composition.** Adam Perkins, representing Heart of Brevard, asked that the organization be included in a two-phase task force process; the item was pulled from this agenda and did not come up for a vote.
**Loitering ordinance framing.** Council members debated at length whether the ordinance targets homelessness or general quality-of-life behavior, per the meeting recording. Aaron Baker warned against "cruelty as policy," while Lauren Weise argued the issue affects housed and unhoused residents alike (52 percent of related calls involved non-unhoused individuals, per Chief Wentzell, as heard on the recording).

#### Public comment
**Peter Offen** urged caution against criminalizing civil offenses, warned that criminal records create housing barriers, and raised privacy concerns about license plate reader cameras.
**Rodney Locks** asked for clarification on loitering definitions, alcohol permits outside the social district, and city versus county concealed-carry rules.
**Doug Powell** argued that firm enforcement of laws against disruptive behavior is compassionate and warned Brevard's tourism economy could suffer without it.
**James Beal** raised concerns that Bracken Trail construction cut off vehicle access to six of nine properties on Pinnacle Road and asked council to review the eminent domain process.
**Adam Perkins** asked that Heart of Brevard be included in the Municipal Service District Task Force process.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
Chief Wentzell committed to bringing back a budget amendment identifying specific funding sources for the Cashers Valley sewer extension, expected around August 17 (verify). She also committed to quarterly and annual reports on Chapter 42 enforcement, and to improved CAD data collection distinguishing unhoused from housed contacts.
City Manager Wilson Hooper committed to coordinating with the county on appraisals, cost estimates, and feasibility analysis for the old courthouse.
Open questions from the recording include: how the city will define "habitual loafing" for enforcement, where the firearms-prohibited city properties are located, and whether the county will proceed with old courthouse redevelopment while the city's feasibility study is underway.

#### Money
**Cashers Valley Road Sewer Extension:** $88,400 from city sources, funding source to be finalized. This amount also satisfies the required match for the city's HUD ProHousing grant application submitted the same night.
**Rosenwald home repair:** up to $1,000,000 (as much as $100,000 per home for 10 homes) proposed in the ProHousing application.
**County courthouse maintenance needs:** estimated around $2,300,000, cited during the courthouse discussion, per the meeting recording (verify).

## Cross-cutting threads

**Old courthouse future use.** The city's August 3 letter of interest in the old courthouse as a potential city hall follows county commissioners' June 22 discussion of what to do with the building once the new $38 million courthouse opens around 2028. Both bodies are now moving toward a joint feasibility conversation, per commitments recorded on both sides.

**Cashers Valley Road / Rosenwald housing.** The city's $88,400 sewer extension commitment and its HUD ProHousing grant application connect to county utility extension funding discussed at the July 13 county meeting, where a Dogwood Foundation grant and DEQ at-risk funds were also in play for the same corridor.
