## Brevard City Council

### November 16, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Agenda and October 19 minutes.** Both approved unanimously, per the agenda packet.
- **Fund 82 project amendment.** Council unanimously approved transfers of $47,500 to the General Fund and $41,369 to the Water/Sewer Fund, and a matching FY 2026-2027 budget amendment, totaling $88,900.
- **Architectural firm selection for Co-Ed Cinema renovation.** Council unanimously approved the staff-recommended designers.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Three UDO and zoning items.** Public hearings were held on the Georgia Hill Street rezoning (NMX to GR-8), the Chapter 16 vesting period amendment, and the Chapter 5 glazing and fenestration amendment. Council did not vote at this meeting; a vote is expected in December after Planning Board recommendations were presented on all three.
- **ETJ modification request.** A request to remove a parcel at 55 Grey Havens Drive from the city's extraterritorial jurisdiction was discussed under unfinished business. Assistant Planning Director Aaron Bland said he had not seen an ETJ removal happen in his 15 years with the city.
- **Smokestack demolition cost overrun.** Staff flagged a projected $75,000 to $100,000 additional cost. The city manager is to bring a recommended funding strategy and budget amendments at the next meeting.

#### Public comment
- No public comments were recorded at this meeting on the rezoning, vesting period, or glazing and fenestration items, per the agenda packet.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- City Manager Wilson Hooper is to bring a recommended funding strategy for the smokestack cost overrun to the next meeting.
- Staff is to prepare a capital projects investment list showing where the city has invested over the last ten years, by location, ahead of a February 2027 strategic planning session.
- Staff is to provide fee schedule transparency, with estimates to development applicants within 10 business days.

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### November 23, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Cedar Mountain House bond resolution.** Approved unanimously, per the meeting recording, clearing the way for the Public Finance Authority to issue up to $70 million in tax-exempt bonds, with $15 million allocated to this facility and roughly $12 million of that toward the purchase price.
- **Courthouse budget modification and contract award.** Approved unanimously, per the meeting recording, awarding construction to Blaine Construction with a total project budget of $44.9 million, including a $34.9 million negotiated base bid.
- **Firefighter radio amplifier system, alternate three.** Approved unanimously at $100,000, per the meeting recording.
- **Board of Health reappointments.** Beth Privitt and Sandra McNeil were reappointed unanimously, per the meeting recording.
- **COSSUP grant transition.** Federal peer support services grant moved to the North Carolina Alliance of Public Health Agencies, per the meeting recording.
- **Consent agenda and meeting agenda.** Both approved unanimously, per the meeting recording.

All vote counts above come from Vimeo auto-generated captions and are unconfirmed pending approved minutes.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Capital projects portfolio.** Staff presented an update showing roughly $220 to $230 million in county capital projects underway, with about $120 million from federal and state grants, per the meeting recording. No board action was taken on this presentation.
- **Landfill expansion.** Staff reported the project has a permit to operate, information has been submitted to DEQ, and the county is awaiting final clearance, with stormwater issues still being worked through, per the meeting recording.

#### Public comment
- **Brian O'Neil, Commissioner-elect,** asked about other counties' approval processes for similar bond financing, due diligence on the entities involved, ALG's litigation and regulatory history, the $70 million statewide bond allocation, and whether commissioner approval was required for the purchase to proceed. Staff responded that a resolution is required for tax-exempt bond issuance, and Grant Melton of CASHF said the bond process covers six other communities and that ALG is retained only as a third-party manager, not as parent company.
- **Owen Carson, Commissioner-elect,** asked about enforceability of affordability commitments, effects on existing residents' care and agreements, management and staffing changes, the split of the $15 million allocation between purchase and reserves, protections if the borrower faces financial distress, and whether approval creates ongoing county obligations. Staff said there are no ongoing county obligations; Grant Melton said affordability is a goal rather than a legally enforceable requirement, and that a 12-month debt service reserve plus 60 to 90 days cash on hand will be funded.
- **Doug Powell** spoke on Thanksgiving themes, thanking the armed forces and outgoing commissioners and reflecting on faith, morality, law, education, and liberty as foundations of freedom. The chair responded, "Happy Thanksgiving."
- **Owen Carson, Commissioner-elect,** separately thanked outgoing commissioners Jake Dalton and Chase McKelvey for their service and said he looked forward to filling the seat.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- Specifics of ALG's litigation and regulatory issues were not fully addressed at the meeting, per the meeting recording.
- Whether affordability commitments for Cedar Mountain House will be legally enforceable beyond the stated goal of maintaining Medicaid community status remains unanswered.
- Whether the county has any oversight mechanism if Cedar Mountain House faces financial distress after approval was not addressed.
- County Manager Jaime Laughter is to keep the board updated on courthouse steel procurement delays and to track UDO compliance costs on the courthouse project on an ongoing basis.
- County Manager Jaime Laughter is to provide a 1-800 VA number for veteran services on the county website, and a legislative goals booklet to board members within two to three weeks.
- Beecher Allison, Education Capital Projects Manager, is to continue coordinating with Bloom Construction and LS3P to keep Brevard High School and Rosman school projects on their December and January timeline milestones.

"I won't say it was always fun," said outgoing Commissioner Jake Dalton, per the meeting recording.

#### Money
- **Cedar Mountain House bonds.** Up to $70 million aggregate ceiling, $15 million allocated to this facility, about $12 million toward purchase price, a projected $12,000 annual property tax loss to the county, all per the meeting recording and unconfirmed pending minutes.
- **Courthouse project.** $44.9 million total budget, $34.9 million base construction bid, $2.9 million for architecture and commissioning, $7 million in owner items and soft costs, $1.2 million in UDO compliance costs, $61,600 in permitting and fees, and a $100,000 firefighter radio amplifier alternate, all per the meeting recording and unconfirmed pending minutes.
- **County capital projects overall.** Roughly $220 to $230 million underway, with about $120 million from federal and state grants, per the meeting recording.
- **Federal PILT payment.** $4.4 million to the county, based on about 89,000 acres of federal public land, per the meeting recording.

## Cross-cutting threads

**Large capital project financing under scrutiny in both bodies.** The city is absorbing a smokestack demolition cost overrun of $75,000 to $100,000 with a funding strategy still pending, while the county just approved a $44.9 million courthouse construction contract and a bond resolution enabling up to $70 million in financing for Cedar Mountain House. Both bodies are carrying major, only partly resolved capital financing questions into December.
