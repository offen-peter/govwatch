## Brevard City Council

### July 20, 2026, regular meeting

Minutes for this meeting became available in this reporting period, confirming figures previously known only from the meeting recording.

#### Decided
- **Sight triangle and stormwater ordinances.** Council unanimously adopted amendments to UDO Chapters 9, 19, 6, and 17 and Code Chapter 38 covering sight triangles and stormwater management development regulations, per approved minutes.
- **Wastewater treatment plant capital project.** Council approved an $8,575,000 capital project amendment for the wastewater treatment plant upgrade under NCDEQ funding agreement SRP-W-134-0175, per approved minutes.
- **Deaver Road rezoning.** Council approved rezoning two parcels on Deaver Road to PGX, Ordinance 2026-33.
- **Trail naming and food vendor pilot.** Council named the fourth Bracken Preserve trail "Mountain Rush Trail" and adopted a food vendor pilot program with a $100 permit fee, running August 1 through December 31, 2026.
- **ABC Board.** Council reappointed Lisa Conner to the ABC Board.

#### Public comment
- **Doug Powell** commended city officials who read the Declaration of Independence on July 4.
- **Rodney Locks** asked council to develop a plan ensuring safe voting access in Brevard and the county.

#### Money
- HUD PROHousing grant application materials cited $10,000,000 for affordable homes on Cashers Valley Road, $400,000 for Rosenwald home repair preservation, a $600,000 in-kind city land donation, and a $400,000 Dogwood Health Trust grant for the county's utility extension. Application was due August 3.

### August 3, 2026, regular meeting

Sourced from the meeting recording; names and figures are unconfirmed as heard on the recording.

#### Decided
- **Cashers Valley Road sewer extension funding.** Council approved an $88,400 funding commitment from city sources for the sewer extension, with the specific funding line to be finalized at a later meeting, as heard on the recording.
- **Loitering ordinance finalized.** Council adopted the loitering, trespassing, and public intoxication ordinance with a change to Section 42-5(C) requiring that conduct "obstruct or interfere with free passage," rather than relying on undefined "loitering" language, as heard on the recording.
- **Old courthouse feasibility.** Council approved sending a letter of interest to the county regarding a feasibility study on using the old courthouse as a future city hall.
- **Parks committee appointment.** Council appointed Shauna Powell to the Parks, Trails and Recreation Committee.

#### Public comment
- **Peter Offen** warned against criminalizing civil offenses that create barriers to affordable housing, and urged caution on license plate reader cameras over privacy concerns.
- **Rodney Locks** sought clarification on the loitering ordinance's "habitual loafing" language and on concealed carry rules on city versus county property.
- **Doug Powell** argued firm enforcement of disruptive-behavior laws is compassionate and necessary, citing an example from Ventura, California.
- **James Beal** raised concerns that the Bracken Trail's construction cut off vehicle access to properties on Pinnacle Road and asked council to examine the eminent domain process.
- **Adam Perkins**, representing Heart of Brevard, asked to be included in the Municipal Service District Task Force process; the related agenda item (M1) had been pulled before the meeting.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- City Manager Wilson Hooper committed to bringing back specific funding-source options for the $88,400 Cashers Valley sewer commitment at the next meeting.
- No timeline was given for the old courthouse feasibility study, including cost estimate or completion date.
- Police Chief Christy Wentzell committed to quarterly and annual reporting on ordinance enforcement outcomes, but no first report date was set.

#### Money
- $88,400 committed for the Cashers Valley Road sewer extension, source unresolved.
- Up to $1,000,000 in HUD funding sought for Rosenwald home repairs (10 homes at up to $100,000 each).
- County courthouse maintenance needs estimated at $2,300,000 (verify), cited in the courthouse feasibility discussion.

### August 17, 2026, regular meeting

No video was retrieved for this meeting. Reporting here relies on the posted agenda and a Transylvania Times news article; neither is a full transcript or approved minutes.

#### Heard, not decided

#### Decided

#### Public comment

#### Unanswered questions and commitments

#### Money

- Potential fines of $25,000 per day cited for city non-compliance with a DEQ grease mandate if the FOG ordinance is not adopted.
