## Brevard City Council

### July 20, 2026, regular meeting
#### Decided
Per approved minutes: Council adopted the Sight Triangle Regulations amendment (UDO Chapters 9, 19; Code Chapter 38) and the Stormwater Management Development Regulations amendment (UDO Chapters 6, 17, 19; Code Chapter 38). Council approved a $8,575,000 capital project amendment for the wastewater treatment plant upgrade (Ordinance 2026-32), rezoned two Deavor Road parcels to PGX (Ordinance 2026-33), named a fourth Bracken Preserve trail "Mountain Rush Trail," adopted the Food Vendor Pilot Program with a $100 permit fee (Resolution 2026-38), and reappointed Lisa Conner to the ABC Board (Resolution 2026-39). Oaths of office were administered to Deputy Chief Alan Bonanno and Sergeant Tanner Green. The loitering/trespassing/public intoxication ordinance received a public hearing but no vote.

#### Money
$8,575,000 wastewater treatment plant upgrade (NCDEQ funding plus city capital funds). HUD PRO Housing grant application described: $10,000,000 for affordable homes on Cashers Valley Road, $400,000 for Rosenwald home-repair preservation, a $600,000 in-kind city land donation, and a $400,000 Dogwood Health Trust grant routed through the county for utility extension.

#### Public comment
Doug Powell spoke on compassion and enforcement toward homelessness. Rodney Locks asked council to develop a plan ensuring safe voting access in Brevard and the county.

### August 3, 2026, regular meeting
Evidence note: based on the meeting recording (YouTube auto-captions), so names, amounts, and vote counts are unconfirmed unless otherwise marked.

#### Decided
Council approved an amended agenda (removed the Municipal Service District Task Force item, added the Cashers Valley Road sewer item), approved April 10 budget workshop minutes and July 20 regular minutes, and approved the consent agenda. Council approved a funding strategy committing $88,400 to the Cashers Valley Road Sewer Extension, with specific funding sources to be finalized later. Council adopted the loitering/trespassing/public intoxication ordinance (Code Chapter 42) with a floor amendment narrowing Section 42-5(C) to require obstruction or interference language. Council approved a letter of interest to the county on an old-courthouse feasibility study for a possible future city hall, and appointed Shauna Powell to the Parks, Trails and Recreation Committee. The record also lists the site triangle and stormwater ordinances as "adopted" again at this meeting, duplicating the July 20 minutes; which meeting represents the operative adoption date is unclear from what was supplied (see Gaps).

#### Money
$88,400 committed to the Cashers Valley Road sewer extension, sources to be set at a later meeting (per the meeting recording). HUD ProHousing grant application submitted the same evening ahead of the 11:59 PM deadline.

#### Public comment
Peter Offen warned against criminalizing civil offenses affecting unhoused residents and urged caution on license plate reader cameras. Rodney Locks asked for clarification on loitering, alcohol-permit, and concealed-carry language differences between city and county property. Doug Powell argued firm enforcement of nuisance laws is compassionate and cited a Ventura, California example. James Beal raised access concerns for Pinnacle Road property owners affected by the lower 200 feet of Bracken Trail and asked council to examine the eminent domain process. Adam Perkins, representing Heart of Brevard, asked to be included in the Municipal Service District Task Force process; that item had been pulled from the agenda before he spoke.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
City Manager Wilson Hooper committed to bringing back specific funding-source options for the $88,400 sewer commitment, previously expected around August 17 (verify). It remains unclear whether the sight triangle and stormwater ordinances were finally adopted July 20 or August 3, given duplicate entries in both records.

## Cross-cutting threads
**Cashers Valley Road sewer extension and affordable housing.** The city committed $88,400 on August 3 as part of a funding stack that also includes a $400,000 Dogwood Health Trust grant routed through the county and a HUD ProHousing application seeking roughly $10 million for the Cashers Valley Road homes and $400,000 for Rosenwald preservation. Both city and county funding decisions are still moving in parallel, with the city's own funding-source vote pending as of this period.

**Old county courthouse's future.** The city sent the county a letter of interest on August 3 about a feasibility study for using the old courthouse as a future city hall, a question that connects to the county's ongoing planning (from earlier meetings) around what to do with the building once its own new courthouse opens.
