## Brevard City Council

### October 14, 2026, joint Downtown Master Plan Committee and Parks, Trails & Recreation Committee meeting

This joint session replaced both committees' regular October meetings, per the meeting notice.

#### Heard, not decided
**Ecusta Trail connection to downtown.** Both committees discussed routing the trail into downtown via a bike lane on W. Main St, with straw votes scheduled on the agenda. No tally or recommendation appears in the records.

#### Money
**Design and construction options for the bike lane ranged widely.** Figures discussed included $25,000 to $100,000 for detailed design, $25,000 to $50,000 to restripe and add delineators in-house, $80,000 to $100,000 for the same work by contractor, and $250,000 to $400,000 for a full repave, restripe, delineator and curb-cut package. Fund 83 (Downtown Master Plan Fund) currently holds $35,000 available. The Parks and Recreation Fund balance is $0.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
**DMPIDAC's November meeting needs a new date.** The November 11 session must move due to the Veterans Day holiday; no replacement date was set in the records.

### October 19, 2026, regular meeting

No video was retrieved for this meeting, so the account below rests on the agenda packet, which is not yet confirmed by approved minutes.

#### Decided
**Proclamation and procedural items.** Council unanimously approved a Key to the City proclamation for Joe Smith, the October 5 regular meeting minutes, and an amended agenda moving the ABC Board appointment to consent, per the agenda packet.

**Veterans Day road closure.** Ordinance 2026-42 closes Main St. for the Veterans Day Ceremony on November 11, 2026, from 10 a.m. to 1 p.m., per the agenda packet.

**Streambank restoration exemption.** Resolution 2026-52 grants a Mini-Brooks Act exemption for Headwaters Engineering's $22,500 design of Lamb's Creek and Norton Creek streambank restorations, per the agenda packet.

**ABC Board appointment.** Resolution 2026-54 appointed James Thomas Kilgore to a three-year term on the Brevard ABC Board, per the agenda packet.

**Building height and parking rules.** Ordinance 2026-43 amended UDO chapters 2, 6 and 19 on building height, and Ordinance 2026-44 removed minimum off-street parking requirements under NC Session Law 2026-39, per the agenda packet.

**Carolina Ave. traffic calming.** Council approved up to $14,000 from Fund 82 and six speed humps, four on Carolina Ave. and one each on Washington and McMinn, with an option to study a three-hump alternative on Carolina, per the agenda packet.

**Traffic Schedule process streamlined.** Council accepted the Public Safety Committee's recommendation to remove the public hearing requirement for Traffic Schedule amendments and move them to consent, per the agenda packet.

**One-way pair dropped from Downtown Circulation Study.** Resolution 2026-53 removed the one-way pair option for Broad and Caldwell from further study, following the Downtown Master Plan Implementation and Downtown Advisory Committee's unanimous recommendation and more than 15 speakers opposing it at the October 5 public hearing, per the agenda packet.

**Fund 82 reallocation to Silversteen Park, per the Transylvania Times.** Council voted 3-2 to reallocate a $41,000 surplus from Carver Street work to seed funding for Silversteen Park. Councilwoman Pamela Holder said: "If we don't have seed money, it shows our lack of interest." Councilman Dean Lytle said: "It's not just a playground. It's a gathering place." Councilman Lauren Wise said: "Use the money now. Don't sit on it."

#### Heard, not decided
**Municipal Infrastructure Reimbursement Agreements.** Council held a public hearing on a new Chapter 2-297 allowing developers to be reimbursed for infrastructure tied to the capital improvement plan. No vote is recorded in the agenda packet.

**Burrell Ave. right-of-way.** Council discussed a request to negotiate conveyance of an unused utility and road right-of-way segment. The agenda packet lists the next step as directing staff but records no decision.

**IMD Feasibility Study Grant.** Council discussed applying for the grant, which requires a 20 percent local match of $12,000 to $16,000 and identifying a preferred greenway project. No decision is recorded.

**Low-Income Homeowner Assistance Program.** Council discussed approving the program and accepting a $60,000 Dogwood Health Trust grant, with awards of $750 to $1,500 per Priority 1 applicant. No vote is recorded in this record.

#### Money
Beyond the items above, the packet lists Carver St. supplementary paving at $8,500, utility pole removal at $25,000, and sidewalk/street design at $60,000, all from Fund 82. It also lists $25,000 to $74,969 for a Cashiers Valley Rd. sewer extension tied to affordable housing, up to $74,969 for Silversteen Park Pavilion seed money, and $30,000 for a tax rebate program for homeowners affected by the 2025 revaluation. The city's 2026-2027 budget figures cited are $7.8 million in property tax revenue and $2.7 million in sales tax revenue. A county tourism figure of $188 million (2025 economic impact) also appears in this packet.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
**Flags at half-staff.** The city flew flags at half-staff October 10 through 16 for former Mayor Jimmy Harris. Mayor Copelof said: "Jimmy Harris was our Mayor for 22 years." No further council action tied to this is recorded.

**Parks plan and Silversteen Park funding gap, per the Transylvania Times.** A roughly $400,000 need remains for restrooms and a pavilion at Silversteen Park. A community task force is to develop a funding strategy, and the Parks, Trails & Recreation Advisory Committee is to update the comprehensive parks plan. No timeline is given.
