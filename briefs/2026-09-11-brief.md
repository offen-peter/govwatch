### September 8, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Consent items.** Council approved minutes from the June 5 budget workshop and the August 17 regular meeting, unanimously, per the agenda packet.
- **Proclamations.** Council proclaimed Rise & Shine's 30th anniversary, Constitution Week (September 17-23), and Childhood Cancer Awareness Month, all unanimous.
- **Road closures.** Council approved closures for the Plein Air Festival (September 19) and Halloweenfest (October 24) under Ordinance No. 2026-37, unanimous.
- **Financial depositories.** Council reaffirmed official depositories under Resolution No. 2026-42, unanimous.
- **Remote participation policy.** Council adopted a Remote Participation Policy for appointed boards and committees under Resolution No. 2026-43, unanimous, keeping in-person and quorum requirements for quasi-judicial matters.
- **Downtown committee renamed.** Council renamed the Downtown Master Plan Committee to the Downtown Master Plan Implementation and Downtown Advisory Committee under Resolution No. 2026-44, unanimous.
- **Presbyterian Church lease.** Council authorized the city manager to sign a lease extension to June 30, 2030, unanimous.
- **Presbyterian Church development process.** Council authorized a non-competitive process for selecting a development partner, 4-1, with Holder opposed, per the agenda packet.
- **Mills Avenue culvert.** Council approved a $350,000 capital project amendment for the Mills Avenue Culvert Repair, which includes $254,838.80 in FEMA-reimbursed change orders.
- **FOG ordinance.** Council approved amendments to the Fats, Oils and Grease program under the Unified Development Ordinance and Code of Ordinances, following a public hearing held August 17.
- **Ecusta Trail Advisory Board.** Council appointed Joseph M. Moore as the at-large member, unanimous.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Thomas Street stop sign.** A public hearing was held on adding a stop sign at Thomas Street and West Main Street; staff recommendation left open whether council acts that night or delays to the next meeting.
- **Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension funding.** Staff presented a funding plan drawing on $11,400 for design, $77,000 for city construction contribution, and proposed splits of $29,622 from Fund 35, $25,000 from Fund 82, and $33,778 from the water/sewer maintenance line, but this was tied to the Fund 82 discussion below and not resolved as a separate decided item in the actions list.
- **Fund 82 (Rosenwald) surplus options.** Options discussed included $35,000 for Carver Street sidewalk design, $8,000 to $14,000 for Carolina Avenue traffic calming, and $30,000 for a tax rebate program, against an $88,969 available balance; no allocation decision appears in the actions taken.
- **Automated License Plate Readers.** A $398,299 five-year equipment bundle was discussed following a September 1 public town hall with roughly 54 attendees and 18 speakers; no vote is recorded in the actions taken.
- **Roadless Area Conservation Rule resolution.** Listed as pending council consideration, with no decision recorded.
- **Cascade Ridge LIHTC.** Staff announced 84 affordable housing units will proceed with accepted funding; this was reported as an announcement, not a council vote.

#### Public comment
- **Butch Stiebing** said the soil at the Presbyterian Church site "has been designated as unfit," with few ways to make it fit, per the meeting recording.
- **Carol Hamann** said her son, a biologist, told her to "do anything to keep them from developing that property," per the meeting recording.
- **Doug Powell** quoted Jefferson: "I prefer dangerous freedom over peaceful slavery," per the meeting recording.
- Additional unnamed speakers raised stormwater concerns, property value impacts, and Bracken Trail access regarding the Presbyterian Church proposal, and surveillance, privacy, and corporate-involvement concerns regarding ALPRs, per the meeting recording.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- Staff committed to presenting additional ALPR safeguard details following the September 1 town hall; timing not specified.
- The city plans further town hall sessions in other precincts, with the next one planned for Pisgah Forest in October.

#### Money
- $350,000 capital project amendment for Mills Avenue Culvert Repair, including $254,838.80 in FEMA reimbursement.
- $11,400 design and $77,000 construction contribution proposed for Cashiers Valley Road Sewer Extension, with $29,622 from Fund 35, $25,000 from Fund 82, and $33,778 from the water/sewer maintenance budget line recommended but not confirmed as adopted.
- $88,969 available Fund 82 (Rosenwald) balance under discussion for allocation.
- $398,299 proposed five-year ALPR equipment contract.

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### July 13, 2026, regular meeting

Minutes for this meeting were approved and published August 28, more than six weeks after the meeting.

#### Decided
- **Courthouse project sole source.** Board adopted Resolution 28-2026 declaring certain courthouse project products and services available only from sole source providers, unanimous.
- **September 14 meeting cancellation.** Board approved using the Multipurpose Chambers for family court on September 14 and canceled that day's regular meeting, unanimous.
- **Tax settlement.** Board accepted the 2025 Tax Settlement Report, showing a county levy of $47,467,688.44 and an all-districts levy of $54,330,548.35, unanimous, and approved the 2026 Order to Collect All Real and Personal Property Taxes.
- **Audit contract.** Board approved a FY2026 audit contract with Martin Starnes & Associates at an $83,000 base fee, not to exceed $124,500, unanimous.
- **Cybersecurity platform.** Board approved a multi-year ThreatLocker agreement at a 63 percent discount from list price, an estimated $215,000 in savings, unanimous.
- **DSS contract carryforward.** Board approved carrying forward $114,515.85 from FY2026 to FY2027 and extending the Allies 4 Outcomes staffing contract through June 30, 2027, unanimous.
- **Appointments.** Board made a series of unanimous appointments: Larry Chapman and Teresa McCall as NCACC voting delegates, Samantha Salyards to the Juvenile Crime Prevention Council, Dr. Ben Lamm reappointed to the Nursing and Adult Care Home Advisory Committee, Mark Emory to the Personnel Board, and Steve Warner reappointed to the Transylvania Economic Alliance board.
- **DEQ grant authorization.** Board authorized the county manager and county engineer to revise DEQ At-Risk funding allocations and execute grant resolutions, unanimous.
- **Legislative goals.** Board approved legislative goals for submission to the NC Association of County Commissioners, unanimous.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Leachate Pretreatment Project.** Staff recommended folding this into ongoing landfill planning rather than proceeding with the current design, after DEQ expanded the project scope; no board action recorded on this recommendation.
- **Morris Road Stormwater Project.** Determined ineligible for DEQ grant funding as designed; staff recommended incorporating it as an alternate within the new courthouse project budget, with no board action recorded.
- **Pisgah Labs, Calvert Road, Azalea Avenue sewer extensions and Brevard High School Pump Station.** All reported as in progress, with engineering, matching funds, or construction funding still pending.

#### Public comment
- Roughly 50 people attended the meeting in the Multipurpose Chambers.
- Four citizens spoke on landfill erosion, sediment in Browns Mill Creek, infrastructure delays, and flood prevention.
- **Representative Anna Ferguson** addressed the board on state funding status, saying "the issue was not the result of any error by Transylvania County."
- **Peter Offen** said the board "should act more promptly on major projects and grant-funded initiatives."
- **Owen Carson** urged the board to "view recent challenges as warning signs requiring proactive leadership."

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- Staff agreed to post information on the county website directing residents to NCDEQ contacts for reporting erosion and sediment concerns.
- A joint inspection with NCDEQ, county staff, the contractor, and project engineers was requested to follow up on landfill Phase 7 erosion controls; no date given.
- The Calvert Road Sewer Extension awaits confirmation of the Town of Rosman's matching funds.
- The Azalea Avenue Sewer Extension awaits a memorandum of understanding with the City of Brevard on construction responsibilities.

#### Money
- $312.16 in tax releases and $1,153.69 in refunds approved for late June.
- $114,515.85 carried forward for the Allies 4 Outcomes DSS staffing contract, against an original $135,921.00 authorization and $16,605.14 spent through June 15.
- $47,467,688.44 county-only 2025 tax levy; $54,330,548.35 all-districts levy; $47,352,840.78 collected and $114,847.66 outstanding as of June 30.
- $83,000 base, $124,500 not-to-exceed audit contract with Martin Starnes & Associates.
- Approximately $215,000 in savings from the ThreatLocker cybersecurity agreement.
- Approximately $4,400,000 in new grant awards announced: $2.1 million for Soil and Water Conservation District work and $2.3 million for communications infrastructure.
- Approximately $120,000,000 in total capital grant funding administered by the county, and approximately $55,000,000 secured collaboratively with the City of Brevard and Town of Rosman since 2022.
- $2,000,000 Dogwood Health Trust grant, expenditure deadline December 2028.
- Approximately $7,000,000 combined federal and state DEQ At-Risk Infrastructure Grant funding, construction deadline December 31, 2026, with possible extensions.
