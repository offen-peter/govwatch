### September 8, 2026, regular meeting

No video was retrieved for this meeting and council minutes are typically unreadable scans, so the following is drawn from the agenda packet rather than confirmed minutes.

#### Decided
- **Consent items.** Minutes from the June 5 budget workshop and August 17 regular meeting were approved, along with three proclamations (Rise & Shine 30th anniversary, Constitution Week, Childhood Cancer Awareness Month), per the agenda packet.
- **Road closures.** Council approved closures for the Plein Air Festival (September 19) and Halloweenfest (October 24) under Ordinance No. 2026-37, per the agenda packet.
- **Financial depositories and governance.** Council reaffirmed official depositories (Resolution 2026-42), adopted a Remote Participation Policy for appointed boards (Resolution 2026-43), and renamed the Downtown Master Plan Committee to the Downtown Master Plan Implementation and Downtown Advisory Committee (Resolution 2026-44), per the agenda packet.
- **Presbyterian Church property.** Council authorized a lease extension to June 30, 2030, and voted 4-1 (Holder opposed) to authorize a non-competitive process for selecting a development partner, per the agenda packet; no minutes confirm this vote.
- **Mills Avenue culvert repair.** A $350,000 capital project amendment was approved, including $254,838.80 in FEMA-reimbursed change orders, per the agenda packet.
- **FOG program.** Amendments to the Unified Development Ordinance Chapter 17 and Code of Ordinances Chapter 70 on fats, oils and grease were approved, following an August 17 public hearing, per the agenda packet.
- **Ecusta Trail Advisory Board.** Joseph M. Moore was appointed as the at-large member, per the agenda packet.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Thomas Street stop sign.** A public hearing was held on adding a stop sign at Thomas Street and West Main Street; staff recommendation left open whether council would act that night or delay, per the agenda packet.
- **Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension funding.** A funding plan combining $29,622 from Fund 35, $25,000 from Fund 82, and $33,778 from the water/sewer maintenance budget was presented for deliberation alongside the Fund 82 options item, with no final vote recorded, per the agenda packet.
- **Fund 82 (Rosenwald) surplus.** Options for the roughly $88,969 balance, including a Carver Street sidewalk phase, Carolina Avenue traffic calming, and a tax rebate program, were presented for discussion with no decision recorded, per the agenda packet.
- **Automated License Plate Readers.** A $398,299 five-year equipment contract was presented following a September 1 town hall; council's options are to approve, amend, table, or reject, with no vote recorded in this packet.

#### Public comment
- **Butch Stiebing** raised concerns about the Presbyterian Church property, saying the soil "has been designated as unfit," per the meeting recording captured in the packet materials.
- **Carol Hamann** opposed development on the property, citing her son's advice as a biologist to "do anything to keep them from developing that property."
- **Doug Powell** invoked Thomas Jefferson in opposing what he characterized as encroachments on freedom, without specifying the agenda item.
- **ALPR town hall.** Eighteen speakers raised surveillance, privacy, and corporate-involvement concerns at the September 1 town hall attended by roughly 54 people; individual speakers were not separately identified in the record.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- **ALPR annual review.** Policy commits the Public Safety Committee to review the ALPR program annually if adopted; no adoption vote has occurred yet.
- **Next town hall.** City plans an additional ALPR informational session in Pisgah Forest in October, with no date set.

#### Money
- **Mills Avenue culvert.** $350,000 total, including $254,838.80 in FEMA-reimbursed change orders.
- **Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension.** $11,400 for design and engineering, $77,000 for construction materials and labor, funded from a mix of Fund 35 ($29,622), Fund 82 ($25,000), and maintenance budget ($33,778).
- **Fund 82 balance.** Approximately $88,969 available for surplus allocation options.
- **ALPR contract.** $398,299 for a five-year equipment bundle, not yet approved.

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### July 13, 2026, regular meeting

Minutes for this meeting were approved and published within the current reporting period, roughly six weeks after the meeting itself.

#### Decided
- **Tax settlement and collection order.** The board accepted the 2025 Tax Settlement Report, showing a county levy of $47,467,688.44 and $54,330,548.35 across all districts, and approved the 2026 Order to Collect, unanimously.
- **Audit contract.** A FY 2026 audit contract with Martin Starnes & Associates was approved unanimously, $83,000 base fee, not to exceed $124,500.
- **Cybersecurity platform.** A multi-year ThreatLocker agreement was approved unanimously at a 63 percent discount, estimated to save about $215,000.
- **Allies 4 Outcomes.** The board approved unanimously carrying forward $114,515.85 from FY 2026 to FY 2027 and extending the DSS staffing contract through June 30, 2027.
- **Courthouse project.** Resolution 28-2026, declaring certain courthouse project products and services sole-source, was approved unanimously.
- **September 14 meeting.** The board approved unanimously using the Multipurpose Chambers for family court on September 14 and canceled that day's regular board meeting.
- **Appointments.** The board unanimously appointed or reappointed Larry Chapman and Teresa McCall as NCACC voting delegates, Samantha Salyards to the Juvenile Crime Prevention Council, Dr. Ben Lamm to the Nursing and Adult Care Home Advisory Committee, Mark Emory to the Personnel Board, and Steve Warner to the Transylvania Economic Alliance board.
- **Legislative goals and DEQ authorization.** The board approved unanimously its legislative goals for the NC Association of County Commissioners and authorized the county manager and county engineer to revise DEQ At-Risk funding allocations and execute related grant resolutions.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Leachate Pretreatment Project.** DEQ requirements expanded the project's scope; staff recommended folding it into ongoing landfill planning rather than proceeding with the current design, estimated at roughly $750,000 plus engineering and contingencies.
- **Morris Road Stormwater Project.** Determined ineligible for DEQ grant funding as designed, estimated at roughly $450,000; staff recommended treating it as an alternate within the new courthouse project budget.
- **Pisgah Labs sewer extension.** An engineering contract selected through a qualifications-based process is under review; a memorandum of understanding remains to be finalized.
- **Calvert Road sewer extension.** Awaiting confirmation from the Town of Rosman that matching funds are available.
- **Brevard High School pump station.** Engineering is underway; construction funding authorization has not yet been requested.
- **Azalea Avenue sewer extension.** Engineering continues using Dogwood Health Trust funds; a memorandum of understanding with the City of Brevard on construction responsibilities is still needed.
- **Upper Transylvania Watershed Study.** Engineering is underway; a state scoping meeting has not yet been scheduled.
- **Landfill Phase 7 erosion control.** The county requested a joint inspection with NCDEQ, staff, the contractor, and project engineers, following resident complaints.

#### Public comment
- **Peter Offen** said the board should act more promptly on major projects and grant-funded initiatives.
- **Owen Carson** urged the board to view recent challenges as warning signs requiring proactive leadership.
- **Representative Anna Ferguson** told the board that a funding mix-up "was not the result of any error by Transylvania County."
- Four additional citizens spoke on landfill erosion, sediment in Browns Mill Creek, infrastructure delays, and flood prevention measures; they were not individually named in the record.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- **Erosion reporting.** Staff agreed to post information on the county website directing residents to NCDEQ contacts for reporting erosion and sediment concerns.

#### Money
- **State infrastructure funds.** $10,000,000 in Session Law 2023 funding was reallocated, with $6,000,000 redirected to other regional priorities and roughly $4,000,000 confirmed for Hurricane Helene recovery in the county.
- **New grant awards.** Approximately $4,400,000 announced, split between the Soil and Water Conservation District ($2.1 million) and communications infrastructure ($2.3 million).
- **DEQ At-Risk Infrastructure Grant.** Approximately $7,000,000 in combined federal and state funding, construction deadline December 31, 2026, with possible extensions.
- **Dogwood Health Trust.** A $2,000,000 housing infrastructure grant, with an expenditure deadline of December 2028.
- **Completed projects.** The US 64/Gaia Herbs sewer extension ($4.6 million) and the Brevard-Rosman emergency water interconnect (approximately $900,000) were reported complete; a $50,000 Upper Transylvania Water Expansion preliminary engineering study also concluded.
- **Cumulative grants.** The county reported administering roughly $120,000,000 in capital grant funding overall, with about $55,000,000 secured collaboratively with the City of Brevard and Town of Rosman since 2022.
