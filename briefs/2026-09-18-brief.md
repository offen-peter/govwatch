## Brevard City Council

### September 8, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Presbyterian Church lease and development process.** Council authorized the city manager to sign a lease extension to June 30, 2030, unanimously, and separately voted 4-1 (Holder opposed) to authorize a non-competitive process for selecting a development partner for the property.
- **Mills Avenue culvert repair.** Council approved a $350,000 capital project amendment, funded in part by $254,838.80 in FEMA reimbursement for change orders.
- **Stop sign at Thomas Street and West Main Street.** Adopted unanimously, per the recap in the September 21 agenda packet rather than the September 8 packet itself, which had listed it as still pending.
- **FOG (Fats, Oils and Grease) program amendments.** Adopted unanimously to UDO Chapter 17 and Code of Ordinances Chapter 70, per the September 21 packet's recap of the vote; the public hearing had been held August 17.
- **Ecusta Trail Advisory Board appointment.** Joseph M. Moore appointed as at-large member, unanimous.
- **Housekeeping items.** Council also approved minutes from two prior meetings, proclaimed Rise & Shine's 30th anniversary, Constitution Week, and Childhood Cancer Awareness Month, approved festival road closures (Ordinance 2026-37), reaffirmed financial depositories (Resolution 2026-42), adopted a Remote Participation Policy for boards and committees (Resolution 2026-43), and renamed the Downtown Master Plan Committee (Resolution 2026-44), all unanimous.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Automated License Plate Readers.** Item M-3 was removed from the agenda at Police Chief Wentzell's request before the meeting, following a September 1 town hall where about 54 people attended and 18 spoke. No council vote occurred.
- **Fund 82 (Rosenwald) surplus options and Cashiers Valley Road sewer funding.** Both were discussed as unfinished business but not decided. Staff are to bring follow-up items back "in coming weeks," per the September 21 packet, with no firm date set.
- **Chief Wentzell's remarks on ALPRs.** "Building trust within the community is more important to me than winning the approval of technology," he said, per the meeting recording.

#### Public comment
- **Butch Stiebing** raised soil conditions on the Presbyterian Church property: "The soil has been designated as unfit. Very few ways to make it fit."
- **Carol Hamann** opposed development on the property, citing a family member's biology background: "My son is a biologist and said do anything to keep them from developing that property."
- **Doug Powell** invoked Jefferson in comments on the property or ALPRs (context not specified in the record): "I prefer dangerous freedom over peaceful slavery."
- **Ashley Hart** spoke on ALPRs and civil liberties: "We the people are where it begins and we're also where it ends."
- Eleven community members total addressed ALPR technology, constitutional rights, Rosenwald Fund allocation, stormwater, and parking concerns, per the September 21 packet's recap; only some speakers are individually named in the record.
- Additional comments covered stormwater impacts, property values, and Bracken Trail access tied to the Presbyterian Church project.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- No date has been set for staff to return with a funding plan for Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension or a final menu of Fund 82 options.
- Whether or when the ALPR proposal returns to council is unresolved after its withdrawal.
- Details of how the non-competitive development partner selection for the Presbyterian Church property will proceed have not been specified.

#### Money
- $350,000 capital project amendment for Mills Avenue culvert repair, including $254,838.80 in FEMA reimbursement.
- Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension: $11,400 for design and engineering, $77,000 in city construction materials and labor, with proposed funding of $29,622 from Fund 35 (W/S Capital Reserve), $25,000 from Fund 82 (Rosenwald), and $33,778 from the W/S maintenance capital outlay line.
- Fund 82 (Rosenwald) available balance: $88,969, with discussed options including $35,000 for a Carver Street sidewalk phase, $8,000 to $14,000 for Carolina Avenue traffic calming, and $30,000 for a tax rebate program.
- ALPR equipment bundle: $398,299 over a five-year contract, cited before the item's withdrawal.
