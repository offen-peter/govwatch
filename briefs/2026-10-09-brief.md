## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### August 24, 2026, regular meeting
Minutes for this meeting were approved and became available during this reporting period.

#### Decided
**Consent agenda and multiple grant actions approved unanimously.** The board unanimously approved a large consent agenda (tax items, proclamations, policy updates, grant authorizations) along with a separate $10,000 St. Philip's grant application for the Silver Squirrels Medical Transportation voucher program and up to $25,000 in Soil & Water Special Projects funds to supplement an Emergency Watershed Protection project.

**Communication tower grants accepted.** The board unanimously accepted $2.3 million in grant awards for public safety communication towers and allocated a $397,860 county contribution from assigned fund balance.

**Connestee Fire Rescue contract amendment approved.** The board unanimously approved a contract amendment moving to monthly reimbursement based on documentation, for a minimum of 18 months.

**Comprehensive Transportation Plan adopted as modified.** The board adopted the plan unanimously, substituting the Wilson Road Extension for the Railroad Avenue Connector.

#### Public comment
**Samantha Jones** addressed the board regarding the investigation into her son's death and questioned a delay in the 911 call.

**Ashley Hart** raised concerns about the Flock Safety automatic license plate reader system, including data retention, data sharing, and lack of a public transparency portal, and said "support for law enforcement does not require endorsing all surveillance technology."

**Robert Jazowski** raised Fourth Amendment concerns and cited examples of ALPR misidentification elsewhere.

**Doug Powell** spoke on Constitution Week and the founders' Fourth Amendment concerns regarding writs of assistance.

**Karen Gleasman** requested a formal written board statement on the county's data center position following Commissioner Dalton's departure.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
Commissioners McKelvey and Chapman stated they were open to hearing from the Sheriff on Flock camera use, including data-sharing arrangements, but no commitment to a hearing date was recorded.

#### Money
Notable figures from the quarterly financial report: $76.6 million in FY2026 General Fund revenues against $72.7 million in expenditures, and a $29,600 unassigned-fund-balance allocation for Tyler Technologies financial software upgrades, including a public-facing Open Finance platform.

### September 28, 2026, regular meeting
This account combines the published agenda with the Vimeo auto-caption transcript. Vote counts and dollar figures drawn from the recording are unconfirmed and are marked accordingly; approved minutes are not yet available.

#### Decided
**Telecommunications tower variance approved, per the recording.** The board approved, by voice vote recorded as unanimous on the recording, a variance allowing a 330-foot Duke Energy tower against the county's 180-foot ordinance limit. This followed a quasi-judicial hearing; the Planning Board had unanimously recommended denial on August 20 while stating it would support the variance if it had authority to grant one.

**Courthouse financing given preliminary approval, per the recording.** The board voted, per the recording, to give preliminary approval to negotiate limited obligation bonds for courthouse construction, with an amount cited on the recording as up to $40 million (verify). Final approval is expected at the October meeting, with pricing in November and issuance in December 2026.

**Connestee Fire Rescue contract amendment executed, per the recording.** The board approved amended payment terms moving to monthly reimbursement for a minimum of 18 months, consistent with the August 24 approval.

**Other consent and appointment items, per the recording.** The board approved the consent agenda, reappointed Alan McAlado to the Brevard Board of Adjustment and Appeals, reappointed Eric Rasmussen and Jessica Huey to the Parks and Recreation Commission, confirmed Kevin Shook and Cameron Sexton as designated agents for Helene public assistance projects, and voted to submit federal legislative goal proposals to the NC Association of County Commissioners, all per the recording's unanimous tallies.

#### Heard, not decided
**CDBG-I grant application for Brevard High School sewer.** The county held a public hearing on an application seeking not more than $1 million in CDBG-I funding toward a pump station project with an estimated $1 million funding gap; the application deadline was September 30, 2026, with a decision pending from the state.

#### Public comment
This section draws entirely on the Vimeo auto-caption transcript; names and some figures are unconfirmed.

**Joan Hardy**, identified as an Army Intelligence and Electronics Warfare Agency veteran, asked about tower reliability and maintenance, and separately raised cybersecurity concerns that foreign adversaries could access Flock camera data.

**Janelle Arceneaux**, an adjacent property owner, objected to the tower variance, disputing the applicant's fall-distance calculations and raising propane tank safety and aesthetic concerns.

**Peter Often** asked for clarification that courthouse bond payments, though secured by property rather than a general obligation pledge, would come from tax dollars; he also argued that commissioners could compel a public process on Flock cameras even without budget control over the sheriff.

**Owen Carson** criticized the county's earlier decision not to retain a prior $10 million state grant for the high school sewer project, raised landfill sediment runoff concerns into Browns Mill Creek and the French Broad River, and separately reframed hazard mitigation grant properties as community opportunities rather than tax-revenue burdens.

**Ashley Hart** detailed Flock transparency portal figures (per the recording: 13 cameras, 122 searches in 30 days, 1,251 hot-list hits, 30-day retention, sharing with 2,612 agencies) and said a camera had been pointed at Democratic Party headquarters for about a year and a half, calling the placement "wrong."

**Doug Powell** argued AI mass surveillance violates Fourth Amendment rights and offered copies of Orwell's "1984" to the board.

**Jason Ball** requested a public hearing from the Sheriff on the Flock system and suggested prioritizing deputy pay over camera funding.

**Robert Jazowski** said he moved from New York partly to escape a surveillance state, and said he does not condone destruction of cameras but wants legal removal.

**David Morrow** thanked commissioners for the new audio-visual equipment, though he said the font is still sometimes too small.

**An unidentified speaker** cited an April 2025 commissioner meeting slide referencing "AI in law enforcement" and warned about predictive policing.

**Commissioner Chase McKelvey** responded during the comment period, noting Flock has positive uses such as child-abduction recovery, and cautioned against a "doom and gloom" framing.

**Vice Chair Larry Chapman** said the Sheriff is an independently elected official outside commission control except on budget, and said "one man with courage makes the majority. I stand with the Constitution," per the recording.
