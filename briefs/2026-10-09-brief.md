## Brevard City Council

### August 17, 2026, regular meeting
Minutes for this meeting were retrieved during this period, filling a prior gap.

#### Decided
- **Festival road closures.** Council adopted Ordinance 2026-37 unanimously, closing roads for the Plein Air Festival (September 19) and HallowenFest (October 24).
- **Financial depositories.** Council adopted Resolution 2026-42 unanimously, reaffirming official financial depositories.
- **Presbyterian Church lease extension.** Council unanimously authorized the City Manager to sign a lease extension on the Presbyterian Church property through June 2030.
- **Development partner selection process.** Council voted 4 to 1, with Holder opposed, to authorize a non-competitive process for selecting a development partner for the church site.
- **Remote participation policy.** Council adopted Resolution 2026-43 unanimously, allowing appointed board and committee members to participate remotely with 24-hour notice and roll call votes.
- **Downtown Master Plan Committee renamed.** Council adopted Resolution 2026-44 unanimously.

#### Public comment
- **Butch Stiebing** objected to the Presbyterian Church site becoming, in his words, "a planned high-rise apartment complex."
- **Carol Hamann** urged preservation of the site's greenspace, calling it home to "animals, birds, trees and vegetation."
- **Lauren Wise** spoke in support of inclusive housing, saying "everyone is allowed to be in Brevard as far as I'm concerned."
- **Ken Hynes** asked about business owner costs during the FOG program public hearing.
- **Doug Powell** raised Fourth Amendment concerns about surveillance cameras during general public comment.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- **Greenway Residential Development proposal.** DFI is to complete an assessment of the developer's plan, financials, and past projects in roughly one month, with a possible third-party review to follow if the assessment is favorable.
- **ALPR town hall.** A public town hall on automated license plate readers was set for September 1, 2026 at MCJCCC, with a further informational session planned for Pisgah Forest in October; no specific date was given.

#### Money
- **Presbyterian Church site study.** The city has spent $11,000 on a completed geotechnical study, with a possible third-party review estimated at $10,000 to $15,000.
- **FOG program penalties.** Civil penalty tiers for the Fats, Oils and Grease program range from up to $500 (Tier 2) to $10,000 to $25,000 (Tiers 6 and 7).

## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### August 24, 2026, regular meeting
This meeting is documented by approved minutes, the strongest evidence tier.

#### Decided
- **Consent agenda.** Board unanimously approved a large consent agenda covering prior minutes, tax actions, proclamations, policy updates, and several grant and operational items.
- **Public Transportation Program application.** Board unanimously approved an FY2028 application with NCDOT totaling $166,689 administrative, $125,000 capital, and $110,000 operating funding, with a $92,503 local match.
- **St. Philip's grant.** Board unanimously authorized a $10,000 grant application for the Silver Squirrels Medical Transportation voucher program.
- **Soil and Water EWP funding.** Board unanimously approved use of up to $25,000 in Special Projects funds to supplement Emergency Watershed Protection engineering costs.
- **Health Department write-off and fees.** Board unanimously approved a $286.20 bad debt write-off and adjusted flu vaccine fees to $25 standard and $125 high-dose.
- **Comprehensive Transportation Plan.** Board unanimously adopted the plan as modified, substituting the Wilson Road Extension for the Railroad Avenue Connector.
- **FY26 Q4 budget amendments and financial report.** Board unanimously approved budget amendments 60 through 77 and received the fourth quarter financial report.
- **Tyler Technologies upgrade.** Board unanimously approved $29,600 from unassigned fund balance for financial software upgrades including a new public-facing Open Finance platform.
- **Communication tower grants.** Board unanimously accepted $2.3 million in grant awards and allocated a $397,860 county contribution from assigned fund balance for public safety communication towers.
- **Appointments.** Board unanimously made several appointments, including Lisa Sheffield, Amber Webb, and Shane Hooper to the Personnel Board, and Sierra Lawing as Review Officer.
- **Connestee Fire Rescue contract.** Board unanimously approved an amendment moving to monthly reimbursement based on documentation for a minimum of 18 months.

#### Public comment
- **Samantha Jones** addressed the Board regarding an investigation into the death of her son and questioned a delay in the 911 call.
- **Ashley Hart** raised concerns about the Flock Safety license plate reader system, including data retention and sharing without a public transparency portal.
- **Robert Jazowski** raised Fourth Amendment concerns and cited examples of ALPR misidentification elsewhere.
- **Doug Powell** spoke on Constitution Week and founders' Fourth Amendment concerns regarding writs of assistance.
- **Karen Gleasman** requested a formal written Board statement on the county's data center position following Commissioner Dalton's departure.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- **Connestee Fire Rescue amendment.** County Attorney was to prepare the formal amendment for Board approval, carried through to the September 28 meeting.
- **Flock camera oversight.** Commissioners McKelvey and Chapman stated openness to hearing from the Sheriff on Flock camera use, including data-sharing arrangements, with no date set.
- **Joint School Bond Project Committee.** Committee needs to begin planning the next phase of bond work, including reviewing Axias reports, ahead of a second bond issuance by fall 2028.

#### Money
- **General Fund.** FY2026 revenues of $76.6 million against expenditures of $72.7 million, with a $4 million anticipated surplus.
- **Solid Waste Enterprise Fund.** Net gain of roughly $730,000 for the year.
- **Education Capital Fund.** $130 million in active funding tied to the school bond program.

### September 28, 2026, regular meeting
This meeting is covered only by the pre-meeting agenda and the Vimeo auto-captioned recording. No approved minutes exist yet, so all votes, names, and dollar figures below are unconfirmed per the meeting recording.

#### Decided
- **Telecommunications tower variance.** Board voted unanimously, per the recording, to approve a 330-foot Duke Energy tower against the county's 180-foot ordinance limit, following a quasi-judicial hearing.
- **Consent agenda.** Approved unanimously per the recording.
- **Board appointments.** Alan McAlado was reappointed to the Board of Adjustment and Appeals, and Eric Rasmussen and Jessica Huey were reappointed to the Parks and Recreation Commission, per the recording.
- **Connestee Fire Rescue contract.** Board executed the amended payment terms discussed in August, per the recording.
- **Helene public assistance agents.** Board confirmed Kevin Shook and Cameron Sexton as designated agents for FEMA public assistance projects, per the recording.
- **Courthouse bond financing.** Board gave preliminary approval, unanimous per the recording, to negotiate limited obligation bonds of up to $40 million (verify) for courthouse construction, with final approval expected at the October meeting.
- **Federal legislative goals.** Board approved submission of federal legislative goal proposals to NCACC, due October 2, per the recording.

#### Heard, not decided
- **CDBG-I grant application.** A public hearing was held on the county's application, not to exceed $1 million, for Brevard High School sewer and pump station improvements; the application deadline was September 30 and the outcome was not yet known.
- **Rosman sewer and water CDBG-DR application.** Staff indicated a letter of support would be on the next agenda for an $8 million request toward an $18 million total project.
- **Hazard mitigation acquisitions.** Eleven properties remain under review; nine in one group are preparing acquisition proposals while six were notified of ineligibility.

#### Public comment
- **Joan Hardy** asked about tower reliability and maintenance during icing, drawing on past county icing problems; Duke's engineer responded citing a TIA-22-H design standard.
- **Janelle Arceneaux**, a nearby property owner, opposed the tower variance, disputing fall-distance calculations and propane tank safety distances, and argued only a 70-foot difference from the water tower did not justify the variance.
- **Peter Often** asked for clarification that courthouse bonds, while secured by property, would be paid from tax dollars.
- **Owen Carson** criticized the county's earlier decision not to retain a $10 million state grant, warning of risk to students if the Brevard High School pump station fails.
- **Joan Hardy** separately raised cybersecurity concerns about Flock data being accessible to foreign adversaries.
- **Ashley Hart** cited the sheriff's transparency portal data (13 cameras, 122 searches in 30 days, 1,251 hot list hits, sharing with 2,612 agencies) and said a public records request for audits was twice denied.
- **Doug Powell** compared Flock surveillance to COVID-era overreach and cited Orwell's 1984.
- **Jason Ball** asked for a sheriff-led public hearing on Flock and suggested deputy pay should be prioritized over camera funding.
- **Robert Jazowski** said he moved from New York to escape surveillance culture and objected to characterizing Flock as reading plates only, given its AI identification features.
- **Owen Carson** also raised landfill sediment runoff into Browns Mill Creek and the French Broad River, asking the county to pause landfill work and reinstate the Transylvania Natural Resources Council.
- **David Morrow** thanked the Board for new audio-visual equipment, noting the font was still sometimes too small.
- **Peter Often** separately detailed Flock data-sharing scale in neighboring counties and noted a camera had been pointed at Democratic Party headquarters for roughly 1.5 years.
- **Ashley Hart** called for removal of the camera pointed at party headquarters, saying no equivalent camera targeted Republican headquarters.
- **Ashley Hart**, in a further comment, reframed hazard mitigation acquisition properties as community assets such as farms, pocket parks, or solar sites rather than tax losses.
- **An unidentified speaker** referenced a 2025 commissioner slide on "AI in law enforcement" and warned of predictive policing risks.
- **Commissioner Chase McKelvey** responded that Flock has legitimate uses, such as child recovery, and cautioned against "doom and gloom" framing.
- **Commissioner Larry Chapman** said opposition was to the tool, not the sheriff personally, and stated that "when Constitution and law enforcement are at odds, Constitution prevails."

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- **Flock camera data.** How many plates the county's 13 cameras capture, and how many have actually been used in enforcement, remains unanswered.
- **Camera at party headquarters.** Why a Flock camera was pointed at Democratic Party headquarters for about 1.5 years was not explained.
- **Duke tower frequencies.** The specific microwave frequencies and whether they pose any health concern to nearby residents was not addressed.
- **Brevard High School sewer.** If the CDBG-I grant is not awarded, no definitive funding source or construction timeline exists yet.
- **Sheriff transparency.** Whether the sheriff will hold a public hearing or provide further detail on Flock use and safeguards is unresolved.
- County management committed to coordinating with DEQ, the engineer, and the contractor on landfill stormwater issues, on an ongoing basis.
- Finance Director Megan O'Neal committed to providing the Board regular progress reports on the Connestee Fire Rescue contract over an 18-month monitoring period.
- Budget Management Analyst Jennifer Wright committed to submitting CDBG-I materials for public review after the September 30 deadline and responding to any hearing complaints within 15 business days.

#### Money
- **Courthouse bonds.** Up to $40 million (verify), per the recording, with final approval expected in October, pricing in November, and issuance in December.
- **TDA occupancy tax revenue.** $1.97 million (verify), per the recording.
- **Hazard mitigation acquisitions.** Potential annual tax revenue loss of $10,730.08 (verify) if all 11 properties are acquired, per the recording.
- **CDBG-I request.** Not to exceed $1 million for Brevard High School sewer improvements, with a remaining $1 million construction funding gap.
