## Transylvania County Board of Commissioners

### October 26, 2026, regular meeting

This meeting is represented by the agenda and by the meeting recording. Vote counts, names and dollar figures drawn from the recording are auto-captioned and unconfirmed until minutes are approved.

#### Decided
- **Consent agenda.** Approved unanimously, per the recording.
- **Library Board of Trustees.** Yvonne Croca appointed, unanimous, per the recording.
- **Council on Aging.** Jennifer B. Henley appointed; a motion to nominate Tom Sweeney, who also spoke in public comment, died for lack of a second, per the recording.
- **Tourism Development Authority.** Reappointments of Dion Hodgson and Norrie Lebrun, with Jessica Whitmire as chair, approved unanimously, per the recording.
- **NCACC voting delegate.** Commissioner Larry Chapman appointed, unanimous, per the recording.
- **Courthouse financing resolution.** Board approved a resolution authorizing limited obligation bonds for courthouse construction, described on the recording as roughly $40 million (verify), unanimous.
- **Debt service fund.** Board approved Budget Ordinance Schedule B6 establishing a new debt service fund with a $5.5 million transfer from the general fund, described as a transfer of existing funds rather than new money, unanimous per the recording.
- **FY 2027 first quarter budget amendments.** Approved unanimously, per the recording.

#### Heard, not decided
- **School funding shortfall.** Superintendent Lisa Fletcher presented a request tied to a gap described on the recording as $1,840,943 (verify), covering salary, benefits, retirement rate and other cost increases; no action was taken, with direction instead to gather more information before a joint meeting.
- **Pre-K funding debate.** Commissioner Chappell questioned whether pre-K is a county funding obligation at all, arguing K-12 is the statutory responsibility; County Manager Lauder responded that pre-K is a discretionary ability to provide, not a requirement, and noted schools can shift pre-K dollars into Fund Eight for K-12 use.
- **Charter school impact.** Dr. Fletcher said state law requires local dollars to follow students to charter schools and that the district gets no state funding for students who move from homeschool into charter schools; commissioners asked for a breakdown of where departing students actually went.
- **ALPR and Flock camera public hearing request.** Chair McCall said no public hearing will be held, calling camera policy the sheriff's constitutional prerogative as an elected officer and distinguishing county authority from city authority over police equipment; the county attorney had advised the board has no authority to direct the sheriff.
- **Legislative advocacy.** A public commenter criticized the board's joking about a Raleigh trip as out of step with a dire school funding report; Commissioner Dalton and Vice Chair Chapman responded that years of personal relationship-building in Raleigh have produced results, including small-school funding.
- **Monthly capital update.** Beecher Allison reviewed the school bond program, described on the recording as a $50 million bond with roughly $18 million remaining, including the Rosman-area groundbreaking planned for October 29.
- **FY 2027 first quarter financial summary.** Finance Director Megan O'Neal presented revenue and expenditure totals; no action required.
- **Allies for Outcomes quarterly report.** DSS Director Amanda Vanderhoff reported on contract utilization for child welfare staffing; no action required.

#### Public comment
- **Ashley Hart** argued ALPR cameras violate Fourth Amendment protections, citing a federal district court ruling and figures on out-of-state agencies searching North Carolina camera networks, and asked commissioners to ban mass surveillance.
- **Tom Sweeney** presented his qualifications for the Council on Aging seat, citing service on a South Carolina governor's council on aging and local boards; he was later nominated from the floor but the motion died for lack of a second.
- **Robert Czajkowski** challenged child-abduction statistics used to justify surveillance, comparing them to child deaths from car accidents, youth sports and bicycles.
- **Doug Powell** invoked Jefferson, Reagan and Jay to argue commissioners should interpret the Constitution themselves rather than defer to judicial review on surveillance questions.
- **Owen Carson** praised a Blue Ridge Community College award, criticized the board's joking tone about a Raleigh trip given the school funding report, and urged more active legislative advocacy.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- Dr. Fletcher committed to providing, before the joint meeting, a breakdown of how many of the 102 students lost in FY25-26 and the 42 lost in FY26-27 went to charter schools, private schools, or other factors.
- Dr. Fletcher committed to identifying the 12 positions absorbed over the past two years and detailing which ESSER-funded positions remain in the local budget.
- County Manager Lauder and Dr. Fletcher committed to scheduling a joint Board of Commissioners/Board of Education meeting in early January 2027 to present all funding options.
- Commissioners were invited to send funding-related questions to County Manager Lauder over the coming weeks for compilation before that joint meeting.
- Commissioner Chapman had earlier suggested a formal letter to state representatives on courthouse funding; whether it was sent is unresolved.
- Final project budget for the new courthouse remains undetermined, expected in November 2026 per county reporting.

#### Money
- **School funding gap.** Described on the recording as $1,840,943 (verify), composed of an $857,000 local need and a $984,000 state shortfall.
- **Courthouse bonds.** Limited obligation bonds described as roughly $40 million (verify), repayment over 20 years.
- **Debt service fund.** $5.5 million transfer from the general fund to establish Fund 43.
- **Q1 FY27 general fund.** $19.9 million in revenues against $16.7 million in expenditures, per the quarterly financial summary.
- **Allies for Outcomes contract.** $45,664 spent of $114,515 authorized for DSS child welfare staffing in Q1.
- **Tax rate.** A roughly 2-cent increase is projected as necessary to cover the current-year gap and set an FY28 baseline, per discussion on the recording.
- **Federal grants.** $2.3 million in federal emergency communications radio tower grants and $2.5 million in STRAP/EWP grants for stream bank repair were noted as approved.
