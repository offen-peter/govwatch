## Brevard City Council

### November 12, 2026, Downtown Master Plan Committee
The committee's regular meeting, originally set for November 11, was rescheduled to November 12 at 3:30 p.m., then cancelled entirely before it convened. The next regular meeting is set for December 9, 2026, at 3:30 p.m. The agenda queued for this session had listed discussion of the Co-Ed Theater update, progress on grants for Downtown Master Plan implementation, and parking study results; none of that was taken up.

### November 16, 2026, regular meeting

#### Decided
- **Agenda and minutes.** Council approved the November 16 agenda as presented and the October 19, 2026 minutes, both by unanimous vote per the agenda packet; this is unconfirmed by minutes or a recording, as no video was retrieved for this meeting.
- **Fund 82 budget amendment.** Council approved an $88,900 Fund 82 project amendment, transferring $47,500 to the General Fund and $41,369 to the Water and Sewer Fund, unanimous per the agenda packet and unconfirmed by minutes or video.
- **Co-Ed Cinema architect selection.** Council approved the recommended architectural firm for the Co-Ed Cinema renovation, unanimous per the agenda packet and unconfirmed by minutes or video.

#### Heard, not decided
- **Georgia Hill Street rezoning.** A public hearing was held on rezoning 84 Georgia Hill Street from NMX to GR-8, with a Planning Board recommendation provided; Council is expected to vote in December.
- **UDO Chapter 5, glazing and fenestration.** A public hearing was held on proposed amendments; Council may adopt, revise, reject, or table the amendment in December.
- **UDO Chapter 16, vesting period.** A public hearing was held on the proposed vesting period amendment; a vote is expected in December.
- **ETJ modification, 55 Grey Havens Drive.** Council took up as unfinished business a request to remove this parcel from the city's extraterritorial jurisdiction. Assistant Planning Director Aaron Bland said, "I have not seen ETJ removal happen in the 15 years I have been at the City." No vote is recorded; next step is for Council to approve, approve with conditions, deny, or table.
- **Smokestack demolition cost overrun.** City Manager Wilson Hooper said, "We project $75,000 to $100,000 additional cost for the smokestack demolition project." No funding decision was made; the manager is to bring a recommended funding strategy and budget amendments at the next meeting.

#### Public comment
No public comments were recorded at the November 16 hearings on the rezoning, vesting period, or glazing and fenestration items, per the agenda packet.

#### Unanswered questions and commitments
- Staff committed to provide fee schedule transparency, with cost estimates to development applicants within 10 business days.
- The City Manager is to bring a recommended funding strategy and budget amendments for the smokestack cost overrun at the next meeting.
- Staff is to prepare a list showing where the City has invested in capital projects over the last 10 years by location, for the February 2027 strategic planning session.

## Cross-cutting threads

- **November 7 election results touch both the county commission and the school board.** Democrats won two commissioner seats, and Lanier and Kelley won school board seats, per Transylvania Times reporting. Commissioner-elect Carson said, "We've got to get on the landfill posthaste. This is probably the single largest cost that we have." A commentator, O'Neill, said the results send "a strong message from the voters" and that "the county is going to be facing additional financial pressures." On the school board side, outgoing member Owen said "the board is going to face very tough decisions ahead," a similar fiscal-pressure framing to the one applied to commissioners.
- **Affordable housing appears in both city and county records this period.** The city's Fund 82 amendment includes $41,369 for a Cashiers Valley Road sewer extension tied to affordable housing, while county notices list a pending public hearing on the Coastal Affordable Senior Housing Foundation application and a separate notice reports part of the county has been designated an Opportunity Zone, which state officials described as a tool to "position federal, state, local, and private partners to work together" (Stein) and to "offer incentives to attract community development" (Burton Hodges).
