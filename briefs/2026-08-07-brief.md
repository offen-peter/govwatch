## County: Board of Commissioners

### What Happened

The Board's most recent meeting was **July 13, 2026**, just before this reporting window opens. No minutes have been approved yet; the only accounts are the agenda and the Vimeo-recording transcript, so all vote counts below are as heard on the recording, not confirmed official tallies.

Per the meeting recording, the Board:
- Approved a modified agenda adding a landfill erosion control update.
- Approved the consent agenda, including the courthouse project sole-source resolution and cancellation of the September 14 meeting to accommodate Family Court use of the chambers.
- Made several appointments, including Vice Chair Larry Chapman as NCACC voting delegate and Mark Emery to the Personnel Board.
- Approved the legislative goals list as presented.
- Extended the Allies for Outcomes DSS staffing contract and carried forward $114,515.85 (per the meeting recording) from FY26 to FY27.
- Accepted the 2025 tax settlement report and approved the 2026 tax collection order.
- Approved a $124,500 (per the meeting recording) FY26 audit contract with Martin, Starnes & Associates.
- Approved a multi-year cybersecurity contract with ThreatLocker, with onboarding to begin in August.

A substantial portion of the meeting concerned landfill sedimentation and a $10 million infrastructure funding dispute. County Manager Jaime Laughter and staff maintained that DEQ grant compliance requires extensive documentation and that recent sedimentation in Browns Mill Creek traces to residential construction upstream, not the landfill. Public commenters disputed this characterization (see Public Comment below).

### Public Comment, July 13 (per the meeting recording)

- **Peter Often** raised concerns about ambiguity in the $10 million funding situation and a pattern of the county sitting on allocated funds while inflation erodes their value, citing the school bonds and the 2019 courthouse funding. He requested a public timeline. Quote: "This is a continuous pattern."
- **Jacob Swope**, a new Pisgah Forest homeowner, criticized the Board for delayed spending decisions, 20 years of courthouse discussion, wage levels causing staff vacancies, and reduced advisory committees.
- **Doug Powell** commended commissioners for reading the Declaration of Independence at the July 4 Main Street event and argued that rights are endowed by the Creator, not government.
- **Owen Carson** spoke twice. First, using a "rumble strip" metaphor, he urged the Board to engage proactively on revenue uncertainty and infrastructure demands. Second, he challenged the landfill presentation directly, citing a 2023 photo of milky discharge and calling for forensic soil testing; Chair McCall ended his second comment when the time limit was reached.

Chair McCall's response to the funding dispute, per the recording: "Facts matter. The facts are on the county website. Facts matter."

### Unanswered Questions and Commitments (July 13, per the recording)

- How much sediment actually reached Woodruff Creek from landfill events remains undetermined; no forensic soil analysis has been commissioned.
- Whether the county has formally approached the City of Brevard about accepting landfill leachate as the city upgrades its wastewater treatment plant is unanswered.
- County Manager committed to posting DEQ sedimentation contact information on the county website after the meeting.
- DSS Director Amanda Vanderhoff committed to quarterly reports on Allies for Outcomes contract usage.
- IT Department is working on a secure method to allow Proton Mail through the county's cybersecurity platform.

## City: Brevard City Council

### What Happened

**August 3 regular meeting.** Per the meeting recording (YouTube auto-captions, names and figures unconfirmed unless otherwise sourced), Council:
- Amended the agenda to remove the Municipal Service District Task Force item and add the Cashers Valley Road sewer extension, driven by a same-day HUD ProHousing grant deadline.
- Approved minutes from the April 10 budget workshop and the July 20 regular meeting.
- Adopted, on second reading, the sight triangle and stormwater management ordinance amendments that had their public hearings July 20.
- Approved a funding strategy for the Cashers Valley Road sewer extension at $88,400 (verify), per the meeting recording, but the specific funding source was not finalized. Council members disagreed on drawing from the Rosenwald fund versus the maintenance budget; Councilman Lidle asked staff to return in two weeks with full options.
- Adopted an amended Chapter 42 ordinance on loitering, trespassing, public intoxication, and hours of operation. The amendment, moved by Councilman Baker, narrowed the loitering clause to require actual obstruction of passage, addressing liability concerns raised by both Baker and Chief Wentzell.
- Voted to transmit a letter of interest to Transylvania County regarding a feasibility study of the old courthouse as a potential city hall site.
- Appointed Shauna Powell to the Parks, Trails, and Recreation Committee.

Separately, the agenda packet for August 3 (agenda-sourced, stronger evidence) lists a $1,108,748 construction contract award to Hyatt Pipeline for the Hillview Street Culvert Replacement Project, fully FEMA funded, and an $8,575,000 Wastewater Treatment Plant Upgrade capital project amendment. Some items in that same packet, including the Deavor Road rezoning and the Food Vendor Pilot Program, duplicate actions already taken July 20 and are not new to August 3; this brief does not double-count them.

**Committees within the period, agenda only, no actions recorded:**
- July 27: Public Safety Committee (traffic calming, ALPR/Axon contract review) and Finance and HR Committee (remote participation policy, Ecusta Trail Advisory Board applications).
- August 5: Public Works and Utilities Committee (stormwater master plan, Hillview culvert, water treatment plant renovation, FOG code changes revisited).

### Public Comment, August 3 (per the meeting recording)

- **Peter Offen** warned against ordinances criminalizing conduct that creates barriers to affordable housing eligibility, and opposed license plate reader cameras on privacy grounds.
- **Rodney Locks** asked for clarification on multiple pending ordinances, including the definition of loitering and rules on concealed weapons on public property.
- **Doug Powell** argued that enforcement of quality-of-life laws is a compassionate response to homelessness, citing Ventura, California as a cautionary example.
- **James Beal** raised concerns that Bracken Trail construction eliminated vehicle access for nine Pinnacle Road properties and asked the city to investigate its eminent domain process.
- **Adam Perkins** asked that the Heart of Brevard organization be included in Phase 1 of the Municipal Service District task force process, given its 30-year downtown partnership.

### Unanswered Questions and Commitments (August 3, per the recording)

- Where residents can find a precise definition of "loitering" and "habitual loafing" in city code remains unresolved; the city attorney noted any clarifying amendment is a political decision.
- Chief Wentzell committed to new CAD clearance codes, officer documentation requirements, and quarterly reporting to Council on loitering and trespassing enforcement, beginning immediately.
- City Manager Wilson Cooper committed to returning within two weeks with specific funding options for the Cashers Valley Road sewer match.
- No timeline was given for the courthouse feasibility study; it depends on coordination with the county.

## Cross-Cutting Threads

**School vestibule funding, resolved at the county level.** The Board of Education's June 15 packet listed the safety vestibule scope change as pending County Commissioner approval. The Board of Commissioners took that up June 22 and approved a $2,184,218 appropriation from the Education Capital Fund Balance for the secure vestibule standard, along with authority to move up to 5 percent between school bond project scopes. This is a completed county action responding to a school request, not a currently open item, but it explains why the schools' June 15 pending item has since been resolved.

**Old courthouse, two bodies now in play.** The county's July 13 consent agenda included a sole-source resolution tied to its new courthouse construction project. Separately, on August 3 the city transmitted a letter of interest to the county about a feasibility study of the **old** courthouse as a potential city hall site. These are two different courthouse-related processes, new construction on the county side and reuse of the old building on the city side, but they now depend on the same county-city coordination. No joint timeline has been set as of this period.
